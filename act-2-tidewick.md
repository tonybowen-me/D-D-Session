# Act II: Tidewick, Where the Tide Keeps Accounts

An 80–100 minute, zero-combat harbor sandbox: a deliberate exhale after three battle-heavy sessions. Tidewick offers warmth, work, gossip, practical preparation, and small human knots to untangle. Nothing here is required for Act III. Generous curiosity simply turns later uncertainty into informed choices.

## The Shape of the Act

**Mood:** Weathered, sociable, salt-stained, historically grounded, and faintly uncanny only at the edges. The supernatural does not dominate daily life; rent, weather, rot, credit, kinship, and the next catch do.

**Zero-combat promise:** No scene in Tidewick needs initiative, threats, weapons, or violence. A failed check costs time, pride, a few coppers, or access to a clue—not hit points. If players introduce force, townsfolk withdraw, shutters close, and Harbor Reeve Anja calmly insists they leave weapons peace-bound at the shrine. De-escalation, apology, restitution, and useful work always reopen play.

**Recommended runtime:**

- **0–10 minutes:** Arrival, orientation, first invitations.
- **10–55 minutes:** Two locations, one minigame, and freely gathered rumors.
- **55–80 minutes:** One social mystery or side path comes into focus.
- **80–95 minutes:** Bell-at-low-water convergence, rewards, and Act III preparation.
- **95–100 minutes:** Departure image and final choice.

If the party meanders happily, let them. The timeline below moves around them and brings useful NPCs into shared spaces.

## What Tidewick Is

Tidewick stands where a cold river spreads into a gray-green estuary. The oldest buildings sit on a natural gravel rise around Saint Orra’s spring; later generations drove oak piles into tidal mud and laid a working quay over them. Houses are timber-framed with limewashed wattle, steep thatch or red clay tiles, and stone footings robbed from an older coastal watchtower. Wealth is displayed not in gems but in sound roofs, brass scales, imported pepper, and rope that has never touched bilgewater.

The harbor follows credible working rhythms. Flat-bottomed boats unload near high water. Fish are sorted and sold quickly, gutted at slate tables, salted in barrels, or hung over slow alder smoke. Nets dry aloft rather than on crowded lanes. Hemp is combed and twisted along a straight ropewalk. Pitch, lamp oil, salt, lime, flour, and timber are stored apart where sparks and vermin can be managed. The tide—not a clock—governs labor.

Tidewick has about 430 permanent residents and perhaps eighty more in fishing season. Everyone knows who owes whom a favor. Most adults have at least two trades because the sea does not promise a living.

## Navigable Layout

Distances are short. From the West Gate to the tide stairs is a five-minute walk unless market traffic delays you.

```text
                              NORTH / UPRIVER
                    [8 Ropewalk & Chandlery]
                           || long hemp yard
 [West Road]--[1 Gull Gate]--[7 Net Loft]--[9 Shipwright Yard]
                   |              |                 |
            [2 Old Rise]------[3 Market Spine]--[10 East Slip]
             Shrine/Spring       Fish Market        Boatyards
                   |              |                 |
             [4 Soot Lane]----[5 Harbor Quay]--[11 Beacon Pier]
            Bakery/Smokehouse     Customs Shed      tide bell
                   \              |
                    \--------[6 Eelhook Inn]
                              |
                        [12 Crooked Step]
                         Oddities Shop
                              |
                      MUD FLATS AT LOW TIDE
                              SOUTH / SEA
```

**Route logic:**

- **Gull Gate → Old Rise:** Dry cobbles, residences, spring, shrine; respectable and quiet.
- **Old Rise → Market Spine:** Downhill through handcarts and baskets; busiest public route.
- **Market Spine → Harbor Quay:** Broad ramp with gutters carrying scales and washwater seaward.
- **Quay → Eelhook Inn:** Under a covered arcade; easy even in rain.
- **Market → Net Loft → Ropewalk:** Narrow “Twine Cut,” steep stairs, strong hemp smell.
- **Quay → Shipwright:** East along the water past timber stacks; muddy at low tide.
- **Inn → Crooked Step:** A descending alley exposed only above half tide; locals use it, carts cannot.

### District Sensory Palette

**Gull Gate and Old Rise:** Chalky limewash, wet wool, rosemary from kitchen boxes, springwater over stone. Gulls sound distant here. At midday, schoolchildren recite tide tables at the shrine. By evening, chimney smoke settles low and doorways glow amber.

**Market Spine:** Silver fish on black slate, scarlet gills, shaved ice where anyone can afford it, baskets creaking, auction calls overlapping. Morning smells clean and briny; midday adds vinegar, onion, and fish blood; after the market closes, lye soap and buckets of seawater dominate.

**Soot Lane:** Alder smoke, toasted rye, hot iron oven doors, malt, and sweet buns. Morning is bakery-warm; afternoon is smokehouse-thick enough to taste; evening carries cooling bread, damp ash, and laughter from workers paid for the day.

**Harbor Quay and Beacon Pier:** Tar, salt, horse urine, kelp, wet oak, ringing rigging, and hollow knocks beneath the decking. High water brings boat voices level with the quay. Low water reveals green-black piles, sucking mud, and crabs ticking under planks. At dusk, harbor lamps make every puddle look like beaten copper.

**Ropewalk and Net Loft:** Dry grass, hemp dust, beeswax, old cork floats. Fibers whisper over workers’ leather aprons. In rain the district is quiet beneath long sheds; in late sun every suspended net casts a second town of diamond shadows.

**Shipwright Yard and East Slip:** Fresh-cut oak, pine shavings, hot pitch, adze blows, and slow saws. At low tide, hulls lean in timber cradles like sleeping animals. At night, one watch lamp burns blue behind oiled horn.

**Crooked Step:** Damp stone, fennel, dust, old paper, and whatever incense Mistress Wisp is trying to sell. When the tide rises, water murmurs beneath cellar grates. Near sunset, glass floats in the oddities window catch red light and seem briefly full of fire.

## Arrival

### Read Aloud

> The road crests a chalk-white rise, and Tidewick appears below—not so much built beside the water as negotiated with it. Roofs descend in close red and brown steps toward a forest of masts. Beyond them, the estuary lies broad and pewter-gray, cut by the black backs of fishing boats returning on the tide.
>
> The town reaches you first by sound: gulls quarreling over offal, an auctioneer singing numbers, the wooden heartbeat of a shipwright’s mallet, and somewhere beneath it all a bell that rings once, waits, and rings again.
>
> Then come the smells. Salt and hot rye bread. Alder smoke. Tar, apples, wet rope, and the clean metallic edge of fish scales. At the gate, a board bears three notices: **LOW WATER AT FIFTH BELL. NO OPEN FLAME BELOW THE MARKET. DEBTS MAY BE PAID IN LABOR BY AGREEMENT.**
>
> A girl in a sealskin cap darts around you with a basket of silver sprats. “Visitors!” she calls downhill. “Visitors with road dust and money—or stories, if the money’s gone!”
>
> Tidewick does not stop to stare. It makes room, which is not quite the same thing.

If the party arrives from the beach with Mara Venn, she points out the boatyard first and says, “Sela has already chosen tackle for your iron weather. Best not make a prepared woman wait.” Then give three immediate, overlapping invitations:

1. The market auctioneer calls for an impartial hand to settle an odd weighing dispute.
2. The baker’s boy offers hot tide-knots if someone can carry a tray without losing any to gulls.
3. A net-mender looks at the Hearthstone, goes still for half a breath, and asks, “Does that always hum near saltwater?”

## Running Tidewick as a Living Place

Every location visit should include:

- one person doing useful work;
- one piece of overheard talk;
- one sensory change caused by tide, weather, or time;
- one invitation rather than a quest demand.

NPCs do not stand waiting for heroes. If missed, they appear later where their schedule takes them. Clues have at least three sources. Failed checks reveal truth with a complication, delay, or social cost.

### A Note on Olive

Olive is not in Tidewick. No Tidewick NPC has seen her here, expects her here, carries a message from her, or hints that she passed through. If directly asked, honest locals simply say they do not know her; inveterate gossips may ask who she is, but cannot produce a lead.

## Cast of Tidewick

Use eight to twelve of these NPCs in play; the rest keep the town feeling larger than the party’s path. Each entry includes an opening line and deeper beats so conversations can breathe.

### 1. Mara Venn, Fisherwoman and Beach Guide

- **Appearance:** Sun-browned woman in her late fifties, blue headscarf, patched oilskin, a net coil over one shoulder.
- **Voice:** Low, blunt, patient coastal cadence; calls Gannet Nine “iron weather.”
- **Wants:** Protect the spawning beach, get practical help to Pell, and keep Tidewick accurately informed.
- **Fears:** A dramatic lie becoming more durable than a quiet fact.
- **Secret:** At dawn, every knotted line on her boat tightened by itself and one net formed an eye-like oval; she cut it away.
- **Links:** Trusts Sela Rook with every boat she owns; brings exact firsthand reports to Nims Calder; asks Edrin to examine the cut net.
- **Opening:** “Town’s ahead. Olive isn’t there, and your stone’s pull passes well beyond it. Tidewick can still make the road to her shorter.”
- **Deeper beats:** Mara has never met Olive and has no Brackenmill news. She can distinguish what she saw at the beach from what others inferred and will publicly correct any claim that Olive is local.
- **Trust earned by:** Protecting the spawning beach, giving an exact account, helping Pell without treating Tidewick labor as disposable, or admitting uncertainty.

### 2. Harbor Reeve Anja Rusk

- **Appearance:** Tall, early fifties, iron-gray braid through a brass ring, blue reeve’s coat patched neatly at both elbows.
- **Voice:** Low, economical, asks exact questions; never raises it.
- **Wants:** A safe harbor and an accurate record before the spring inspection.
- **Fears:** A preventable accident being excused as “the sea’s will.”
- **Secret:** She quietly pays the shrine’s lamp oil from harbor fines because the council refused the expense.
- **Links:** Older sister to Della Rusk; trusts Sela Rook’s workmanship; publicly disagrees with Brannic Vey about credit.
- **Opening:** “Welcome. Are you tying up, trading, or only taking the town’s measure?”
- **Deeper beats:** She explains that Tidewick’s law treats rescue gear as common property during an emergency. She noticed three new coils of unusually blue-gray twine in customs salvage, all cut to identical lengths. She will not speculate about why.
- **Trust earned by:** Reporting a hazard without exaggeration, helping with the tide board, or accepting that procedure can be an act of care.

### 3. Della Rusk, Fish Auctioneer

- **Appearance:** Broad-shouldered, bright orange scarf, silver scales stuck to her sleeves like sequins.
- **Voice:** Musical, rapid, turns prices into rhyming chants.
- **Wants:** To finish the market with no unsold catch and prove her new apprentices competent.
- **Fears:** Losing her hearing; she already misses high-pitched sounds.
- **Secret:** The disputed market weight is honest; the wooden counter beneath one scale swells in damp weather.
- **Links:** Anja’s younger sister; employs Pip Sedge; owes baker Odo a month of breakfasts.
- **Opening:** “Roadfolk! You look unbiased and insufficiently fish-scented. Perfect.”
- **Deeper beats:** She can map who bought which catch. She heard a thin tone near a sealed customs barrel that no one over forty seemed able to hear.
- **Trust earned by:** Treating apprentices respectfully, solving the scale issue without accusing anyone, or buying the last humble catch.

### 4. Pip Sedge, Market Runner

- **Appearance:** Thirteen, sealskin cap too large, front tooth missing, perpetually carrying a basket.
- **Voice:** Breathless declarations; knows everyone’s business but not its significance.
- **Wants:** To be trusted to ring the auction handbell.
- **Fears:** Deep water seen through gaps in planks.
- **Secret:** Pip swapped two chalk tally marks to help a struggling fisher and now regrets it.
- **Links:** Idolizes Della; is protected by Brother Edrin; trades gossip with Nym Wisp.
- **Opening:** “Money, stories, or a useful pair of hands—which have you brought?”
- **Deeper beats:** Pip saw Kett Vale leave the net loft late with a blue-gray thread on his boot. The thread came from a torn parcel, not theft. Pip can recreate every morning delivery route.
- **Trust earned by:** Taking the fear of plank gaps seriously, keeping Pip’s minor wrongdoing private while fixing it, or letting Pip lead.

### 5. Odo Fenn, Baker

- **Appearance:** Flour-white eyebrows, burn-scarred forearms, magnificent waxed mustache.
- **Voice:** Warm theatrical baritone; describes food like weather.
- **Wants:** His daughter to inherit a debt-free bakehouse.
- **Fears:** A miller’s price rise after poor inland grain harvests.
- **Secret:** His famous “sea salt starter” is ordinary rye starter begun by his late husband; the story keeps customers smiling.
- **Links:** Shares a wall and chimney schedule with Maelin Soot; feeds Della on credit; uncle to Tavi Fenn.
- **Opening:** “Come in before the wind eats you. Bread first; decisions afterward.”
- **Deeper beats:** He talks about rationing ovens, communal baking days, and how honest legends can still be loving ones. A late-night tapping in the chimney is a loose iron damper, not a ghost.
- **Trust earned by:** Helping serve workers before nobles, praising the staff rather than only the food, or sharing a true memory tied to a meal.

### 6. Maelin Soot, Fish Smoker

- **Appearance:** Compact, soot-dark apron, shaved head, amber spectacles, hands stained with brine.
- **Voice:** Dry, smoky laugh; answers in recipes and ratios.
- **Wants:** Permission to replace a dangerous shared flue.
- **Fears:** Fire crossing from smokehouse to bakery.
- **Secret:** She has already bought the bricks on credit and hidden them under alder logs.
- **Links:** Fondly feuds with Odo over chimney hours; buys wood from Sela; aunt to Lio Soot.
- **Opening:** “If you can smell alder, the draft is right. If you can smell pitch, run and fetch me.”
- **Deeper beats:** She can identify woods and oils by scent. A waxed scrap found in her woodpile smells of lampblack, salt, and the same resin used on marine contract seals.
- **Trust earned by:** Taking fire precautions seriously, helping inspect the flue, or admitting ignorance and listening.

### 7. Brannic Vey, Chandler

- **Appearance:** Narrow spectacles, russet waistcoat, fingertips polished by counting waxed cord.
- **Voice:** Precise, faintly nasal; quotes prices to the half-copper.
- **Wants:** Every account settled before his retirement.
- **Fears:** His niece will mistake generosity for poor business.
- **Secret:** He forgives one widow’s lamp-oil debt each winter and records it as “evaporation.”
- **Links:** Employs niece Nym Wisp; argues with Anja over debt law; purchases rope from Ysra Coil.
- **Opening:** “Need light, line, leverage, or an object you did not know had a proper name?”
- **Deeper beats:** He distinguishes tarred marline, sailmaker’s twine, net cord, and decorative braid. The blue-gray sample is waxed against water but carries regular hard knots at finger-width intervals—possibly notation, measurement, or both.
- **Trust earned by:** Paying a fair price without haggling for necessities, spotting a bookkeeping kindness and not exposing it, or organizing his tangled sample board.

### 8. Ysra Coil, Ropewalk Master

- **Appearance:** Powerful forearms, close-cropped white curls, hemp fibers clinging to a black wool dress.
- **Voice:** Slow and rhythmic; pauses as though keeping tension on a strand.
- **Wants:** Apprentices who understand that consistency matters more than speed.
- **Fears:** Imported cheap rope failing under load and being blamed on all ropemakers.
- **Secret:** Her left hand has begun to tremble; she hides it by keeping that hand on the laying hook.
- **Links:** Supplies Brannic; mother of Fen Coil; respects Sela but dislikes her occasional shortcuts.
- **Opening:** “Take three weak things, turn them together properly, and you may trust your life to them.”
- **Deeper beats:** She demonstrates S- and Z-laid rope. The strange twine is not local hemp: it contains a fine metallic filament that sings when tensioned. She refuses to guess its purpose.
- **Trust earned by:** Valuing craft, choosing steady work over showing off, or discreetly adapting a task around her tremor.

### 9. Fen Coil, Net-Mender and Singer

- **Appearance:** Lanky, sea-glass earrings, indigo-stained fingers, one eyebrow split by an old hook accident.
- **Voice:** Speaks softly but sings with clear, carrying resonance.
- **Wants:** To compose a work song that people will still sing in fifty years.
- **Fears:** Silence after gradual hearing loss, which runs in the family.
- **Secret:** Certain notes make the blue-gray twine vibrate visibly; Fen discovered this by accident and has told only Ysra.
- **Links:** Ysra’s child; performs with Lio Soot; shyly admires Brother Edrin.
- **Opening:** “Your stone and my net disagree about the note. May I hear which one wins?”
- **Deeper beats:** Fen can test frequency without defining what it means. At one pitch the twine forms tiny standing waves; at another the Hearthstone may answer according to its established nature. This is foreshadowing, not a motive revelation.
- **Trust earned by:** Joining a song without mockery, protecting Fen’s hearing, or describing honestly what the Hearthstone does.

### 10. Sela Rook, Master Boatwright

- **Appearance:** Square-built, copper-brown skin, close-cut hair, wooden spectacles perched on her head; measures spans with thumb and little finger.
- **Voice:** Clipped and resonant; speaks in measurements, tolerances, and material limits.
- **Wants:** To lift Gannet Nine above the tide without damaging it, then launch the restored rescue yawl *Mercy Bell*.
- **Fears:** Pride causing someone to conceal a weak joint or underestimate a load.
- **Secret:** She keeps a salvaged ceramic bearing collar from an old tide engine that may fit Gannet; she offers it if Pell treats her as a collaborator.
- **Links:** Trusted by Anja and Mara Venn; mentor to Kett Vale; respects Ysra’s rope and buys smoked fish from Maelin.
- **Opening:** “You can tell a sound hull by knocking. People are less obliging.”
- **Deeper beats:** She explains clinker-built hulls, caulking, and why repair marks are part of a vessel’s history. A set of brass fasteners vanished only because the invoice lists them under an obsolete name. Examining Gannet with Pell reveals that her ceramic collar is compatible.
- **Trust earned by:** Credit shared craftsmanship, helping launch the yawl, or admitting and correcting a measurement error.

### 11. Kett Vale, Shipwright Apprentice

- **Appearance:** Seventeen, freckles beneath sawdust, leather measuring thong wrapped twice around one wrist.
- **Voice:** Quick, defensive, becomes eloquent about wood grain.
- **Wants:** Public credit for the rescue yawl’s rib.
- **Fears:** Being dismissed as the child of an indebted family.
- **Secret:** Kett opened a washed-up parcel out of curiosity, found blue-gray twine and waxed papers, then put everything in customs salvage. A thread snagged on a boot.
- **Links:** Apprentice to Sela; cousin of Nims Calder; resents Pip’s gossip.
- **Opening:** “If this is about the missing fasteners, learn the old inventory names first.”
- **Deeper beats:** Kett remembers fragments on the soaked paper: “party of the first part,” “upon due resonance,” and “consideration conveyed.” No names, purpose, or signer survived.
- **Trust earned by:** Asking for Kett’s account privately, ensuring proper credit for the rib, or defending apprentices from casual blame.

### 12. Brother Edrin Vale, Keeper of Saint Orra’s Shrine

- **Appearance:** Brown skin weathered by wind, cream robe under an oilskin cape, keys and a bone whistle at his belt.
- **Voice:** Gentle and formal; pauses before naming supernatural conclusions.
- **Wants:** The town to remember rescue is a duty, not heroism.
- **Fears:** Saint Orra becoming a mascot instead of a moral example.
- **Secret:** Years ago he saw a contract curse whose victim mistook compulsion for desire. He also knows the “saint’s spring” predates the shrine and considers that history more sacred, not less.
- **Links:** Watches over Pip; close friend of Anja; shares evening songs with Fen; examines Mara Venn’s cut net.
- **Opening:** “Drink if you are thirsty. Pray if you wish. Neither requires payment.”
- **Deeper beats:** The shrine keeps rescue records, names of the lost, and copies of old maritime agreements. Edrin can identify infernal-contract residue and explain binding phrases without naming a culprit or motive. Comparing the Hearthstone pulse, shrine cords, and witness-knots lets him identify a true tuning interval.
- **Trust earned by:** Leaving a name or memory in the book, refilling public water jars, or helping without seeking recognition.

### 13. Nims Calder, Innkeeper of the Eelhook

- **Appearance:** Copper-brown curls under a kerchief, sleeves rolled high, smile that assesses before it welcomes.
- **Voice:** Warm, quick, conversational; repeats a speaker’s exact unusual words.
- **Wants:** A lively common room with no one drinking away tomorrow’s food money.
- **Fears:** A proposed quay rent will drive crews to another harbor.
- **Secret:** Nims hosts a quiet mutual-aid purse beneath the third stair tread and anonymously supplies the coastal courier with carefully sourced local news.
- **Links:** Kett’s cousin; employs retired sailor Cob Rill; buys Odo’s bread and Della’s fish; trades verified accounts with Mara Venn and Jory Fen.
- **Opening:** “Beds, bowls, baths, or news? I can provide three honestly and one entertainingly.”
- **Deeper beats:** Nims knows which rumor began at which table and writes conflicting versions on a slate rather than choosing the loudest. Nims can arrange introductions, baths, laundry, secure storage, a private room, or a discreet wake-up at any tide.
- **Trust earned by:** Tipping staff, declining to exploit drunk gossip, or contributing coin or labor to the mutual-aid purse.

### 14. Cob Rill, Eelhook Server and Retired Sounder

- **Appearance:** Seventy, tattooed depth marks on both forearms, one clouded eye, immaculate apron.
- **Voice:** Gravelly and amused; says “mark that” before important facts.
- **Wants:** Someone younger to learn the safe channel by lead-line rather than rumor.
- **Fears:** Charts replacing local observation entirely.
- **Secret:** Cob deliberately exaggerated a “singing mud” story to keep children away from a collapsing tide cave.
- **Links:** Works for Nims; old rival of Ysra; grandfather of Lio Soot.
- **Opening:** “Mark that: visitors look toward the sea; sailors look at what the sea is doing.”
- **Deeper beats:** Cob knows soundings, currents, weather signs, and why a rhythmic echo near the mud flats changes with tide height. Part is natural resonance through old drainage culverts.
- **Trust earned by:** Learning the lead-line chant, respecting a warning, or improving rather than ridiculing his protective story.

### 15. Mistress Calis Wisp, Proprietor of Wisp’s Useful Impossibilities

- **Appearance:** Plum velvet coat gone shiny at the cuffs, black hair threaded with tiny brass keys, magnifying lens on a chain.
- **Voice:** Intimate stage whisper; labels ordinary things magnificently.
- **Wants:** Every object to find the person who will notice its real use.
- **Fears:** Being treated as a fraud when she is merely an enthusiastic curator.
- **Secret:** Half her “mysteries” are mundane beach finds, correctly identified in a private ledger.
- **Links:** Aunt and guardian of Nym Wisp; buys salvage through Anja; exchanges stories with Cob.
- **Opening:** “Nothing here is cursed before purchase, and very little afterward.”
- **Deeper beats:** Calis separates claims from evidence if asked respectfully. She owns a cracked brass listening cup that makes low vibrations easier to feel and a sample of old contract ribbon with knotted witness-codes.
- **Trust earned by:** Choosing an object for use rather than prestige, enjoying a story without insisting it be fact, or asking to see the honest ledger.

### 16. Nym Wisp, Chandler’s Clerk

- **Appearance:** Twenty, ink-stained cuffs, shaved sides with a long dark braid, six pencils behind one ear.
- **Voice:** Deadpan, exact, devastatingly observant.
- **Wants:** To modernize Brannic’s accounts without erasing his humane exceptions.
- **Fears:** Inheriting a shop that cannot survive kindness.
- **Secret:** Nym anonymously wrote the council proposal that turns forgiven debts into a public rescue fund.
- **Links:** Brannic’s niece and Calis’s ward; swaps gossip with Pip; debates tariffs with Anja.
- **Opening:** “If my uncle quoted you the half-copper price, he likes you. Strangers get whole numbers.”
- **Deeper beats:** Nym can compare handwriting and contract forms. The washed paper follows archaic legal structure but was written recently with modern iron-gall ink.
- **Trust earned by:** Solving a ledger puzzle, respecting both compassion and sustainability, or supporting the rescue-fund proposal without naming its author.

### 17. Lio Soot, Lamplighter and Fiddler

- **Appearance:** Lean, red scarf, tarred gloves, fiddle case patched with sailcloth.
- **Voice:** Cheerful tenor; taps rhythms on every surface.
- **Wants:** Enough lamp oil to light Crooked Step all winter.
- **Fears:** A fall on wet quay stairs ending both trades.
- **Secret:** One harbor lamp flickers in exact time with a faint tone from the customs shed, but only when the strange twine is pulled taut.
- **Links:** Maelin’s nephew; Cob’s grandchild; musical partner to Fen.
- **Opening:** “If you’re staying past dusk, learn which puddles are deeper than they look.”
- **Deeper beats:** Lio can reproduce the flicker rhythm: short-short-long, repeated with slight drift. It is an observation, not a decoded message.
- **Trust earned by:** Carrying oil, fixing a stair marker, keeping time in a tune, or taking occupational safety seriously.

### 18. Tavi Fenn, Mudlark and Amateur Historian

- **Appearance:** Eleven, oversized boots, reed basket, coat pockets labeled in chalk.
- **Voice:** Solemn academic phrasing interrupted by childhood excitement.
- **Wants:** One find accepted into the shrine’s history cabinet.
- **Fears:** Adults discarding evidence because a child found it.
- **Secret:** Tavi planted one old button months ago to impress Edrin, confessed privately, and has since become scrupulously honest.
- **Links:** Odo’s niece; studies with Edrin; follows Cob’s tide warnings.
- **Opening:** “Do you possess qualifications in ceramic fragments, or only opinions?”
- **Deeper beats:** Tavi found a clay seal impression bearing no crest, only interlocking loops. It fits neither fishing guild nor harbor office records.
- **Trust earned by:** Cataloguing finds carefully, distinguishing “unknown” from “magical,” or treating the prior mistake as corrected behavior.

### 19. Hesta Brine, Mussel Seller and Council Elder

- **Appearance:** Tiny, layered skirts, hands like driftwood roots, pearl-headed cane used only for emphasis.
- **Voice:** Razor-sharp, enjoys strategic misunderstandings.
- **Wants:** The new quay rent debated in public, not decided over private supper.
- **Fears:** Tidewick becoming picturesque for visitors and unlivable for workers.
- **Secret:** She owns the Eelhook building and has never raised Nims’s rent.
- **Links:** Political opponent but personal friend of Anja; former employer of Della; spars verbally with Calis.
- **Opening:** “Are you important, useful, or still deciding?”
- **Deeper beats:** Hesta remembers older contract customs: knotted ribbons once let illiterate witnesses verify page order and terms. She recognizes structure, not parties or intent.
- **Trust earned by:** Asking workers how policy affects them, speaking plainly, buying small shellfish rather than demanding the finest catch.

### 20. Jory Fen, Coastal Courier

- **Appearance:** Dust-red riding coat, cropped black hair, inkproof satchel strapped high, tired bay mare waiting at the harbor post.
- **Voice:** Brisk and cheerful; confirms every name and destination twice.
- **Wants:** Leave before sunset with accurate paid messages and no avoidable ambiguity.
- **Fears:** Becoming part of a mystery instead of carrying its record.
- **Secret:** Jory’s route does not reach Brackenmill directly; it joins an inland relay that does. A warning may arrive before or after the party.
- **Links:** Receives sourced town accounts from Nims; trusts Mara Venn’s eyewitness precision; drinks one departure tea with Edrin.
- **Opening:** “Recipient, destination, return address—twice each, please. Urgency is not improved by misspelling.”
- **Deeper beats:** Jory has no direct report from Olive or Brackenmill. Jory can carry a warning to the relay, preserve a sealed copy of observed contract language, or take a corrected account before rumor leaves town.
- **Trust earned by:** Giving exact wording, distinguishing fact from theory, paying the ordinary rate without trying to purchase impossible speed, or correcting a public rumor.

## Playable Minigames

Each takes 5–12 minutes. State stakes first. Let players choose the ability and describe how it helps; suggested checks prevent every game from becoming Dexterity-only. On failure, award partial information or a lesser prize whenever possible.

### 1. Della’s Honest Weight

**Premise:** Two sellers accuse each other after identical baskets produce different readings.

**Play:** The group gets **3 investigation actions** before the next auction bell. Each action uses a different approach:

- inspect scale and weights: Intelligence (Investigation) DC 12;
- test level, moisture, and counter: Wisdom (Perception) or tool proficiency DC 12;
- calm sellers and reconstruct sequence: Charisma (Persuasion) DC 12;
- improvise a known counterweight or lever test: Strength or Intelligence with artisan’s tools DC 11.

**Outcome:** Two successes reveal that the damp counter has swollen beneath one scale foot. Three successes also uncover Pip’s altered chalk tally without publicly exposing Pip.

**Stakes:** Failure means Della refunds both parties from her own purse and loses face; the party can still repair the counter through labor.

**Prize:** **Market Favor**, 5 sp of provisions, and the thin-tone rumor from Della. With three successes, Pip becomes a guide and shares the delivery map.

### 2. Ropewalk: Keep the Lay

**Premise:** Ysra lets visitors help make a short rescue line.

**Play:** A three-stage team challenge; one character may handle each stage, and no character needs Dexterity.

1. **Comb fibers:** Strength (Athletics), Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), or weaver’s tools DC 11.
2. **Maintain tension while walking backward:** Constitution (Athletics), Wisdom (Insight into Ysra’s rhythm), or Performance DC 12.
3. **Lay strands evenly:** Intelligence (Investigation), Dexterity, or any relevant artisan’s tools DC 13.

Each success adds 1 **Quality**. A character can gain advantage by following Ysra’s cadence instead of racing. At 2–3 Quality the line is sound; at 1, Ysra repairs it while teaching why it failed; at 0, it becomes decorative cord and everyone laughs kindly.

**Stakes:** Entry is 2 sp each or one hour’s promised labor.

**Prize:** At 2 Quality, a 25-foot rescue line and **Ropewalk Favor**. At 3, Ysra tests the strange twine and identifies its metal filament. At 0–1, participants still receive cord bracelets and advantage on their next knot-related check from the lesson.

### 3. Lead-Line Sounding Chant

**Premise:** From a moored boat, Cob teaches the rhythm used to sound depth and read the bottom.

**Play:** Four calls in sequence. Cob says the depth; players must answer with the proper rhythm or choose checks:

- remember marks: Intelligence (History) DC 11;
- feel the line strike bottom: Wisdom (Survival) DC 12;
- keep chant under harbor noise: Charisma (Performance) DC 12;
- hold position against current: Strength (Athletics) DC 12.

Track **Course** from 0. Success moves +1; failure moves −1. Reach +2 before −2 to “guide” the imaginary vessel safely. Clever use of a staff, familiar, spell, or teamwork grants advantage if it preserves the peaceful training frame.

**Stakes:** The losing side buys cider, maximum 1 sp total. No one falls in.

**Prize:** A marked sounding line, Cob’s safe-channel sketch, and knowledge that the mud-flat “singing” changes with culvert depth. In Act III, the sketch or lesson can grant advantage on one navigation, current-reading, or depth-estimation check.

### 4. Gulls and Tide-Knots

**Premise:** Carry twelve hot buns from bakery to market while the town’s boldest gulls make their attempt.

**Play:** Start with **12 buns**. Cross three zones:

1. Soot Lane awnings,
2. Market Spine crowd,
3. open quay.

At each zone, one player leads with a distinct solution: Dexterity (Acrobatics), Wisdom (Animal Handling), Charisma (Performance or Intimidation directed theatrically at birds), Intelligence (Nature), Strength to shield the tray, or a harmless spell. DCs 10, 12, and 13. Success loses none; failure loses 1d3 buns; an especially funny plan converts one lost bun into applause.

**Stakes:** Only buns and dignity. Players may buy replacements at 2 cp each.

**Prize:** Deliver 9+ for free lunch and **Bakehouse Favor**; all 12 earns Odo’s rescuer packets and Maelin’s confidence about the flue scrap. Deliver fewer and Odo serves the survivors anyway.

### 5. Knot-and-Note at the Net Loft

**Premise:** Fen sings short phrases while players tie matching patterns into cord: rhythm translated into knots.

**Play:** Three rounds, each with a clue and a choice:

- **Round 1, pattern:** Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Perception) DC 11.
- **Round 2, execution:** Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), Intelligence with rope knowledge, or Charisma (Performance) to direct a partner DC 12.
- **Round 3, variation:** Wisdom (Insight), Charisma (Performance), or spellcasting ability used for harmless sound/light shaping DC 13.

Gain one **Harmony** per success. A player who sings, taps, signs, or draws rather than ties can participate fully.

**Stakes:** 3 cp entry into the loft tea fund.

**Prize:** At 2 Harmony, Fen shares the vibrating-twine observation. At 3, the party records the reproducible pitch interval: a useful Act III frequency clue. At 0–1, Fen reveals the observation but the party lacks the exact interval.

### 6. Launch the *Mercy Bell*

**Premise:** The restored rescue yawl must move from cradle to water at the right instant.

**Play:** Place three tokens: **Balance, Momentum, Timing**, all neutral. The group gets one attempt at each:

- Balance: Intelligence (Carpenter’s Tools/Investigation), Wisdom (Perception), or Strength DC 12.
- Momentum: Strength (Athletics), Constitution, or Charisma (Performance) to coordinate workers DC 12.
- Timing: Wisdom (Survival), Intelligence using tide tables, or Dexterity to knock wedges on signal DC 13.

Success turns a token favorable. Failure turns it strained. Before launch, one character may sacrifice their personal prize, spend a Favor, or confess a mistake to reset one strained token to neutral. Two favorable tokens launch beautifully; one launches safely but splashes everyone; none means the tide is missed, and Sela reschedules without blame.

**Stakes:** Reputation and one tide cycle, never damage.

**Prize:** **Shipwright Favor**, Kett receives deserved credit, and the party may hire the yawl at half price. A beautiful launch grants one use of trained rescue assistance in Act III if geographically plausible.

### 7. Hesta’s Mussel Court

**Premise:** Hesta stages a mock council argument over the proposed quay rent, assigning visitors positions they may not personally hold.

**Play:** Three speakers each make one argument using different evidence: workers’ testimony, harbor finances, or town history. Use Persuasion, Insight, History, Investigation, or Performance DC 12. A player may skip rolling by stating a concrete compromise that names who pays, who benefits, and how abuse is prevented.

**Stakes:** Public opinion, not binding law.

**Prize:** Two successes earn **Council Favor** and Hesta’s account of witness-knots. A strong compromise also reveals Nym’s rescue-fund proposal without exposing Nym as author.

## Small Social Mysteries and Side Paths

Choose one as the act’s spine. Let another remain as ambient life.

### A. The Crooked Scale

**Question:** Who cheated at the fish auction?

**Truth:** Nobody. Humidity swelled the wooden counter under one foot of the scale. Pip’s unrelated tally alteration makes the situation look worse.

**Clues:** A dry scale balances; a water ring marks the low foot; carpenter’s chalk shows seasonal movement; Della’s records show discrepancies only on rainy mornings.

**Resolution:** Shim the scale, explain the cause without public accusation, and privately help Pip restore the tally by compensating the fisher through market labor.

**Reward:** Market Favor, provisions, Pip’s delivery-route knowledge, and a townwide reputation for fairness.

### B. The Missing Shipwright’s Brass

**Question:** Is an apprentice stealing fittings?

**Truth:** “Six dozen roves” were received, but the new clerk searched for “washers.” They are the same paired riveting fittings in this yard’s old inventory.

**Clues:** No forced lock; weight of the brass bin matches the invoice; Sela’s old ledger uses “rove”; Nym recognizes the vocabulary shift.

**Resolution:** Reconcile old and new inventories, then give Kett public credit for the yawl rib so the theft rumor loses its emotional fuel.

**Reward:** Shipwright Favor, half-price boat service, and a waterproof document tube.

### C. Smoke Between Friends

**Question:** Why are Odo and Maelin suddenly quarreling about chimney hours?

**Truth:** Both know the shared flue is unsafe. Maelin hid purchased bricks because she fears a permit delay; Odo fears confronting her will expose her debt.

**Clues:** Soot streaks above the wrong damper; hot pitch smell only during overlapping use; brick dust beneath alder logs; Anja’s code allows emergency repair with two craft witnesses.

**Resolution:** Inspect the flue, recruit Sela or Ysra as second witness, and negotiate alternating oven/smoke schedules until repair.

**Reward:** Bakehouse Favor, free travel food, Maelin’s waxed contract scrap observation, and a warm sendoff meal.

### D. The Customs Shed’s Thin Note

**Question:** Why do only some people hear the shed whistle?

**Truth:** Blue-gray twine from a washed parcel is stretched between two salvage hooks. Wind through a loose roof board tensions it. Its metallic filament resonates at a high frequency; nearby lamp vibration produces a repeating flicker.

**Clues:** Reports cluster among younger people; sound stops if either hook is touched; Ysra identifies the filament; Fen reproduces the note; Lio records the flicker rhythm.

**Resolution:** Document pitch, knot spacing, and tension; safely slacken and store the sample in waxed canvas. Do not decode purpose or ownership.

**Reward:** Frequency clue, twine sample if Anja formally loans it, and Harbor Favor.

### E. The Saint’s Older Spring

**Question:** Does evidence that the well predates Saint Orra make the shrine a fraud?

**Truth:** The spring was communal long before the shrine. Orra’s remembered act was keeping it public during a siege. The place is older than the story, and the story preserves the same value.

**Clues:** Mason marks below the shrine foundation; Tavi’s pottery dates earlier; Edrin’s uncensored records; Hesta’s childhood account of shared-water customs.

**Resolution:** Create a label for the history cabinet that distinguishes archaeological fact, oral tradition, and present practice without humiliating believers.

**Reward:** Shrine Favor, access to maritime records, and one accurately copied example of old witness-knot contract structure.

## Dynamic Timeline

Shift these beats to fit arrival. The tide is the town’s clock.

### High Water / Arrival

- Fishing boats unload; Market Spine is crowded.
- Della’s scale dispute begins.
- *Mercy Bell* waits on its cradle for the next favorable tide if the party misses this one.
- Anja inspects the customs salvage and hears no note herself.
- Mara Venn delivers her beach report while Sela loads rollers and block-and-tackle for Gannet Nine.

### One Bell Later

- Odo sends buns to market, opening Gulls and Tide-Knots.
- Kett arrives at the chandlery seeking the “missing” brass invoice.
- Pip repeats the blue-thread misunderstanding to Nym.
- Fen crosses the market, notices the Hearthstone, and invites the party to the net loft without claiming to understand it.
- Edrin compares Mara’s cut net with tightened shrine cords, distinguishing contract residue from motive.

### Falling Tide

- Cob begins a public lead-line lesson.
- Tavi searches the newly exposed mud edge under supervision.
- The customs note becomes audible as wind strengthens through the loose board.
- Maelin and Odo’s chimney schedule overlaps; a bad smell prompts concern, not fire.
- Jory Fen arrives at the harbor post. Jory has no direct Brackenmill report and opens the outgoing satchel for one final relay packet.

### Low Water / Fifth Bell

- Harbor work pauses for a short meal.
- Hesta holds Mussel Court at the market steps.
- The old culvert mouths are visible from a safe marked path; their echo can be tested.
- Major NPCs naturally converge: Anja, Edrin, Nym, Fen, and whichever craftsperson the party befriended.

### Turning Tide / Dusk

- Lio lights harbor lamps; one flicker can be compared with Fen’s recorded rhythm.
- Eelhook fills with workers; rumor corrections spread based on party choices.
- Any resolved side path is toasted with watered cider.
- Departure remains possible by road at once or by boat on rising water.
- Jory seals the last packet. An exact warning can depart for the inland relay; otherwise Jory carries only Nims’s sourced public account.

### If Players Do Nothing

The town does not collapse. Della postpones use of the bad scale, Anja stores the twine safely, Odo and Maelin stagger their fires, and Sela delays the launch. Tidewick is competent. The party’s value is perspective, connection, and time—not rescuing helpless locals.

## Reactions to the Hearthstone

Preserve whatever properties the Hearthstone already has. Tidewick residents react to observable behavior, reputation, or the party’s handling of it; they do not suddenly know its origin or function.

- **Anja:** Treats it as potentially hazardous cargo only if it emits heat, sound, or light. Requests facts, not surrender. “What does it do when no one is frightened?”
- **Della:** Uses a fishmonger’s practical scale: “Warm enough to spoil mackerel? Loud enough to upset an auction?”
- **Fen:** Notices tonal interaction. With permission, compares sung pitches and records only repeatable responses.
- **Ysra:** Watches for vibration passing into fiber. She may cradle it on rope loops to isolate its movement.
- **Edrin:** Does not call it holy or profane. Offers a cloth and quiet place if carrying it has become emotionally heavy. He recognizes coercive contract residue, never intent.
- **Calis:** Is delighted, offers twelve unsupported theories, then clearly labels all twelve unsupported.
- **Tavi:** Asks to draw it beside a ruler and include date, weather, and witness names.
- **Pip:** Wants to know whether it has a nickname and whether it can warm a bun.
- **Hesta:** Judges the party by whether they display it for status, hide it from accountability, or handle it as a responsibility.
- **Lio:** Compares any pulse to lamp flicker and taps the rhythm.
- **Nym:** Asks whether custody, ownership, and responsibility are actually the same thing.

**Trust effect:** Open but careful demonstration earns Harbor or Net Loft trust. Refusing politely earns respect. Boasting invites outlandish rumors. Deception causes Calis and Nym to become politely exacting, but does not close the sandbox.

## Act III Benefits Without Making Tidewick Mandatory

Use **Tidewick Favors** as physical tokens: knotted cord loops, stamped shell chits, or written names. The party can earn at most **3 Favors** during this act, even if they help everywhere. Every benefit duplicates something attainable in Act III through ordinary checks, resources, or alternate routes.

### Spend a Favor in Act III

Spend one token for one of these:

- gain advantage on a check involving navigation, tides, rescue, ropes, hulls, contracts, acoustic vibration, or community negotiation;
- declare that the party packed one mundane rescue supply worth 2 gp or less from Tidewick;
- recall one relevant craft fact and ask the DM for a concrete, truthful environmental detail;
- turn a failed noncombat group check into a mixed success with a cost;
- establish a friendly Tidewick contact by messenger if communication is plausible.

### Information That Can Carry Forward

Each clue is useful but nonessential:

- **Contract form:** Recent writing in archaic legal structure; fragments include “party of the first part,” “upon due resonance,” and “consideration conveyed.”
- **Witness-knots:** Knotted ribbon can verify page sequence or witnessed terms for readers and nonreaders alike.
- **Twine:** Blue-gray, waxed, nonlocal fiber with a fine metallic filament and regularly spaced hard knots.
- **Frequency:** Under tension, the twine responds at reproducible pitches; a nearby lamp may flicker in a drifting short-short-long rhythm.
- **Custody record:** The parcel washed ashore and entered customs salvage through Kett and Anja; no sender, recipient, purpose, or motive is established.

Do **not** use these clues to reveal the unknown antagonist’s motive. They foreshadow methods, materials, and contractual language only.

## Pacing Controls

### To Tighten Toward 80 Minutes

- Present only three initial invitations: market scale, net loft note, and bakehouse flue.
- Use eight active NPCs: Mara Venn, Sela, Edrin, Nims, Della, Fen, Kett, and Jory.
- Let each successful minigame reveal two linked clues.
- At 50 minutes, sound Fifth Bell and bring relevant NPCs to Mussel Court.
- Resolve one mystery; summarize the rest as town life continuing.
- At 75 minutes, have Nims offer a final meal and Anja ask what the party wants recorded before departure.

### To Expand Toward 100 Minutes

- Add one craft demonstration and one private conversation after a minigame.
- Run two linked mysteries, such as missing brass → contract vocabulary or scale dispute → Pip’s twine sighting.
- Let players compare the customs frequency at net loft, customs shed, and lamp.
- End with the *Mercy Bell* launch or a public rumor correction at the Eelhook.

### If Energy Dips

- Pip arrives with a contradictory rumor and asks the party to adjudicate.
- A handbell announces five minutes to a public game.
- Change the senses: rain begins on awnings, tide exposes the mud, lamps bloom at dusk.
- Offer a concrete choice: “Follow Kett to customs, help Maelin inspect the flue, or take Cob’s boat lesson?”

### If Players Suspect a Trap Everywhere

State through evidence that Tidewick’s secrets are ordinary human vulnerabilities: debt, pride, fear of blame, private kindness. Let an NPC admit a small secret before asking for trust. Never punish caution; give it something real but proportionate to find.

### If Players Try to Turn It Into Combat

Anja, Edrin, or Nims calmly names the behavior and offers an off-ramp: peace-bind weapons, replace damaged goods, sit for tea, or leave by the west road. No villain appears to justify escalation. A sincere apology and repair restores access.

## Closing the Act

At the turning tide, gather the consequences in one place—the Eelhook, the market steps, or Beacon Pier.

### Read Aloud

> Evening does not quiet Tidewick; it changes the work. Market cries become supper talk. Mallets cease one by one. Along the quay, Lio touches flame to lamp after lamp until the harbor wears a necklace of small amber lights.
>
> The tide turns among the pilings with a sound like someone slowly drawing breath. Nets overhead stir. From Soot Lane comes rye crust, alder smoke, and the bright edge of cider. Somewhere behind you, a dispute you helped settle is already being retold less accurately and more fondly.
>
> Tidewick asks for no oath. It gives you what working towns can give: a dry rope, a remembered kindness, the right tool named at the right moment, and several people who will now be glad if you return.

Ask each player one closing question:

- What small object from Tidewick do you keep?
- Whose work changed how you see the town?
- Which rumor do you correct before leaving?
- What do you write—or decline to write—in Saint Orra’s public book?

Then award up to 3 Favors, confirm material supplies, and name the exact clues learned.

# Compact DM Quick Reference

## Core Promise

**No combat. No mandatory Act III gate. No Olive lead. No antagonist-motive reveal.** Secrets are social, civic, historical, or craft-based. Clues concern contract language, twine, and frequencies.

## 90-Minute Default Route

1. **0–10:** Arrival read-aloud; offer scale, buns, and Fen’s Hearthstone question.
2. **10–25:** Honest Weight or Gulls and Tide-Knots.
3. **25–40:** Free location: chandlery, shrine, inn, shipyard.
4. **40–55:** Knot-and-Note or Ropewalk challenge.
5. **55–75:** Resolve Customs Thin Note, Crooked Scale, or Smoke Between Friends.
6. **75–88:** Fifth Bell convergence; compare rumors and choose rewards.
7. **88–95:** Closing read-aloud, Favors, departure.

## Fast NPC Voices

- **Anja:** Low, procedural care. Wants safety.
- **Della:** Auction-song speed. Wants fairness.

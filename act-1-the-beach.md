# Act 1: The Beach

**Target time:** 25–35 minutes  
**Tone:** Spent relief, uncanny resonance, practical kindness, distant dread  
**Hard continuity:** This begins immediately after the cave recovery and boss fight. The party and the Tinkerer are on the beach. His mecha-machine lies on its back in shallow water while he works its controls. The Resonant Hearthstone pulses in the sand. Olive is **not** in Tidewick: she is very far away, at the Tinkerer's hut in a different town, with her eyes and mouth sewn shut by supernatural twine connected to a hell-contract puppet master. The puppet master's motives are unknown and remain undecided.  
**Encounter rule:** Zero combat. Do not frame hazards, silhouettes, or social tension as fake combat.

## DM Quick Reference

### What this act must accomplish

1. Let the players breathe after the boss fight.
2. Establish that the Hearthstone is active, directional, and linked to a distant suffering presence.
3. Give the Tinkerer a repair scene that reveals character and produces useful choices.
4. Introduce Tidewick through a local fisherwoman at the top of the dune.
5. End with a meaningful choice: investigate locally, seek help in Tidewick, rest briefly, or begin preparing to travel toward Olive.

### Scene clock

| Beat | Time | Essential result |
|---|---:|---|
| Opening and recovery | 3–5 min | Beach state, machine state, pulsing stone |
| Hearthstone investigation | 8–10 min | Three core clues, no dead ends |
| Tinkerer repairs | 6–8 min | Repair status, Olive concern, travel options |
| Fisherwoman on dune | 5–7 min | Tidewick introduced, local omen/aid |
| Choice and transition | 3–5 min | Party selects next approach |

If time runs short, combine the Tinkerer's final repair test with the fisherwoman's arrival. If time runs long, use one optional resonance impression or a short Tidewick question branch.

### Core truths the players cannot miss

- The Hearthstone is pulsing in a deliberate rhythm, not randomly.
- Its strongest pull points inland and far beyond Tidewick.
- It carries an impression of restraint, twine, and a silenced person, but does not identify an attacker or motive.
- Olive is far away at the Tinkerer's hut in another town—not in Tidewick.
- The machine can be made safe here, but full mobility requires time, parts, or assistance.

Whenever a check fails, give the core clue and attach a cost, uncertainty, or complication. Never withhold the only route forward.

## Opening Read-Aloud

> The sea keeps trying to erase what just happened.
>
> Each low wave slides across the beach, fills the scars in the sand, and retreats with a whisper of shell grit. Behind you, the cave mouth is only a black notch in the stone, its violence reduced to distant dripping. Ahead, the Tinkerer's mecha-machine lies on its back in the shallows like some enormous iron castaway. One arm points uselessly toward the clouds. The other disappears beneath green water while the Tinkerer, knee-deep beside its ribs, works a bank of exposed controls with both hands.
>
> Steam sighs from the machine. A lens flickers once—amber, dark, amber—and paints the foam in weak gold.
>
> Then the sand beside you answers.
>
> The Resonant Hearthstone gives a slow, deep pulse. Grains of wet sand jump away from it in a perfect ring. A second pulse follows, close enough to feel through your boots and teeth. Not an alarm. Not quite a heartbeat. More like a distant hand, tugging a taut thread.
>
> High above, dune grass bends in the salt wind. A lone fisherwoman has stopped at the crest with a coil of net over one shoulder. She watches the stranded machine, then the stone, and does not run.

Pause. Ask each player: **“What is the first thing your character checks now that the danger is over?”** Affirm recovery actions, tending wounds, gathering belongings, or simply taking a breath.

## The Beach at a Glance

- **Shallows:** Calm knee-deep water; the mecha-machine is stable but taking saltwater through a damaged hip seal.
- **Wet sand:** The Hearthstone rests where it was set down after the cave. Its pulse makes concentric rings.
- **Caveward:** No pursuit, no lurking threat, and no combat cue. Only settling stone and receding water.
- **Dune crest:** Mara Venn waits in plain view. She approaches only after the party acknowledges her or the machine vents a plume of steam.
- **Beyond the dune:** A footpath reaches Tidewick in about twenty minutes. Tidewick is a coastal settlement, not Olive's location.

## Investigation: The Resonant Hearthstone

Run this as an open investigation. Players may touch, listen, use magic, compare rhythms, inspect the sand, ask the Tinkerer, or try something unexpected. Each useful approach yields a core clue automatically; checks determine precision and extra benefit.

### The three core clues

1. **Direction:** The pulse has a “lean.” It points inland, far beyond the dune and beyond Tidewick.
2. **Condition:** The resonance carries sensory fragments of taut twine, obstructed sight, forced silence, and a living person in worsening distress.
3. **Connection:** The rhythm catches on something associated with the Tinkerer—his tools, machine, or memory of home. He realizes it points toward his hut in a different, distant town, where Olive remained.

Do not present a literal remote view unless the players earn the strongest result. Even then, show Olive's condition, not the puppet master or its motive.

### Graded clue checks

Use the most fitting skill. Suggested options:

- **Arcana or Religion:** Read supernatural resonance.
- **Investigation:** Map pulse intervals, sand rings, and direction.
- **Insight:** Interpret the emotional pressure carried in the pulse.
- **Medicine:** Distinguish living distress from an echo of past pain.
- **Survival:** Establish bearing and approximate distance.
- **Performance or tool proficiency:** Match the pulse as vibration, rhythm, or mechanism.

**DC 10 — Core signal:** The character gets one core clue and knows the effect is present, not merely an old recording.  
**DC 13 — Clear reading:** They get two core clues and notice the pulse worsens by a small degree during the scene.  
**DC 16 — Resonant impression:** They get all three clues plus one vivid, nonvisual impression: coarse twine drawing tight across skin; darkness despite open eyes; an unsounded word caught behind closed lips; or the smell of oil and woodsmoke associated with the Tinkerer's hut.  
**DC 19+ — Controlled echo:** They may ask one question: “Which direction?”, “How far?”, “Is the person alive?”, “Is the bond worsening?”, or “What familiar place is involved?” Answer truthfully but incompletely. The stone cannot reveal identity or motive of the controlling entity.

### Fail-forward outcomes

- **9 or lower:** Give one core clue. The investigator also takes on a harmless echo until the next short rest: fingers feel threaded together, speech briefly feels difficult, or blinking brings a moment of darkness. This is unsettling, not damage or incapacitation.
- **Failed attempt to manipulate the pulse:** The stone goes quiet for ten seconds, then resumes faster. The party learns that forceful probing aggravates or alerts the connection, but no route closes.
- **Conflicting interpretations:** The Tinkerer can calibrate using a brass tuning pin, resolving direction automatically after one minute. The cost is one minute taken from machine repair.
- **Magic that detects evil, curses, or extraplanar influence:** It confirms infernal-contract residue and active coercion, but not the responsible being's identity, location, terms, or purpose.

### Useful experiments

- **Move the Hearthstone:** The pulse weakens seaward and strengthens inland.
- **Bring it near the machine:** A compass needle in the control bank swings toward the Tinkerer's distant home.
- **Speak Olive's name:** The next pulse stutters, then answers twice. This strongly suggests a connection but is not proof of conscious communication.
- **Ask yes/no questions:** The stone is unreliable as an oracle. It responds clearly only to immediate condition and direction; it cannot adjudicate motives.
- **Wrap or bury it:** The physical vibration muffles, but the inward pressure remains. This can buy emotional space, not stop the distant affliction.

## NPC: Pellin “Pell” Arkwright, the Tinkerer

**Appearance:** Compact, salt-soaked coat full of tool loops; silver-brown hair plastered to his forehead; one magnifying lens clipped over his left eye.  
**Voice:** Fast, precise, and dry; technical language when frightened; drops into short, plain sentences when the truth lands.  
**Wants:** Stabilize the mecha-machine, protect Olive, avoid abandoning either responsibility, and turn panic into a solvable sequence.  
**Tell:** Counts under his breath and taps each tool twice before using it. When lying by omission, he polishes an already-clean lens.  
**Secret:** Before leaving, Pell noticed Olive had become uneasy around loose thread and knots. He dismissed it as exhaustion and has not admitted that guilt aloud. He does **not** know the puppet master's motive.  
**What he knows:** Olive remained at his hut in the inland town of **Brackenmill**, many days away by ordinary travel. Tidewick is only the nearest settlement. His machine could shorten the journey if repaired.

### The repair situation

The machine, **Gannet Nine**, is not dying or dangerous. It is flooded, misaligned, and awkwardly pinned by its own weight.

- **Immediate stabilization:** 5–10 minutes. Stops saltwater damage and restores message-lamp, compass, and one diagnostic lens.
- **Walking repair:** Several hours with improvised materials, or 45–60 minutes with a Tidewick block-and-tackle, dry workspace, and replacement seal.
- **Full travel readiness:** Requires further work and a scarce ceramic hip collar. Pell believes Tidewick's boatwright may have a compatible ring. Once fitted—or improvised over several extra hours—Gannet can use the Hearthstone's home-bearing for a **Hearthline Return** to its Brackenmill workshop. This is the default fast transition to Act 3 if the campaign has no established long-distance travel method.

Do not make the repair a dead end. Whichever route the party chooses, they gain something.

### Helping Pell

One character leads; others can grant advantage through a distinct contribution.

**DC 11 Strength (Athletics):** Shift the machine enough to drain its hip housing.  
**DC 12 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) or thieves'/tinker's tools:** Free a salt-jammed control linkage.  
**DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation/Arcana):** Trace the Hearthstone-induced compass oscillation.  
**DC 13 Wisdom (Perception/Survival):** Time the work between waves and keep sand out of the open housing.

**Success:** Stabilization takes 5 minutes and reveals the compass bearing to Brackenmill. Pell can also restore either the message-lamp or diagnostic lens immediately.  
**Failure:** Stabilization takes 10 minutes, and Pell must sacrifice a nonessential brass panel or one spool of conductive wire. The machine is still safe. The player chooses what gets restored, so failure changes resources rather than blocking progress.  
**Creative automatic success:** Leverage, shape water, mending used on a small break, a suitable tool proficiency, or an excellent practical plan can succeed without a roll.

### Pell dialogue branches

**If asked what is wrong with the machine**

> “Hip seal split, control cage flooded, dignity irrecoverable. Gannet will live. Walking is another question.”

**If asked about the pulse**

> “That interval is not the cave. It is catching on my compass—on me, perhaps. Give me the stone. No, gently. It dislikes certainty.”

**When Olive's name becomes likely**

> “Olive stayed at my hut in Brackenmill. Brackenmill is nowhere near Tidewick. If this is reaching from there…”  
> He stops counting. “We establish what we know. Then we move.”

**If confronted about what he withheld**

> “She flinched at thread. Cut every loose cord from her sleeves. I called it nerves because I had a cave to reach and a machine to finish. That was convenient of me.”

Choices:

- **Comfort him:** He admits the loose-thread detail and becomes more open.
- **Press him:** DC 11 Insight or Persuasion; on failure, he still tells the truth but becomes terse and throws himself into repairs.
- **Blame him:** He accepts it without argument. The repair still progresses; later he may take needless risks unless reassured.

**If asked whether Olive is alive**

> “The rhythm changes. Pain changes it. That means living response—or something made to imitate one. I believe she is alive. I will not call belief proof.”

**If asked who did this or why**

> “Infernal contract-work, perhaps. A handler, perhaps. The stone gives us effects, not intent. Anyone claiming motive now is dressing fear as knowledge.”

**If urged to leave immediately**

> “We can. On foot, poorly supplied, leaving Gannet to the tide. Or we spend a little time making the journey faster and gather facts in Tidewick. I will go either way. I would prefer the choice to be ours.”

## NPC: Mara Venn, Tidewick Fisherwoman

**Position:** At the top of the dune, always introduced in open view.  
**Appearance:** Sun-browned woman in her late fifties; blue headscarf; patched oilskin; net coil and a basket containing two silver fish.  
**Voice:** Low, unhurried coastal cadence; asks blunt questions without hostility; calls unfamiliar machines “iron weather.”  
**Wants:** Keep Tidewick informed, prevent strangers from damaging the spawning beach, and help people in obvious trouble without being made a fool.  
**Tell:** Tests every knot in her net while deciding whether she trusts someone. Stops knotting when she hears a lie.  
**Secret:** At dawn, every knotted line on her boat had tightened by itself, and one net formed an eye-like oval. She cut it loose before anyone saw. She does not know what it means.  
**What she knows:** Tidewick has a boatwright, a small shrine, beds, food, a signal tower, and a coastal courier due this afternoon. She has never heard of Olive and knows nothing reliable about Brackenmill's current state.

### Her entrance

Mara raises one empty hand before descending three steps—not close enough to crowd the party.

> “If the iron weather is yours, it has picked the gentlest beach for dying. If it isn't dying, Tidewick has ropes, a boatwright, and people who prefer explanations before smoke.”

### Mara dialogue branches

**“Where are we?”**

> “South beach below Tidewick. Twenty minutes by the dune path, longer if your iron friend needs carrying.”

**“Is Olive in Tidewick?”**

> “Never heard the name. If she's the one your stone is mourning, that pull isn't toward our roofs. It passes us inland.”

State this plainly to protect continuity: **Olive is not in Tidewick.**

**“What help is in Tidewick?”**

> “Sela Rook keeps the boatyard and owns tackle enough to turn a whale. Brother Edrin tends the shore shrine. Nims has rooms and soup. Courier bell rings before sunset if the road is open.”

**“Have you seen anything strange?”**

Mara first says, “Strange arrived on its back in my shallows.” If treated respectfully or shown the Hearthstone:

> “My knots pulled themselves tight before dawn. One net made the shape of an eye. I cut it. Didn't care to have it looking.”

No roll is required if the party shares a meaningful truth. Otherwise, **DC 12 Persuasion or Insight** gets the same account. On failure, she gives the practical Tidewick information now and reveals the omen while turning to leave; no clue is lost.

**“Can you send word?”**

> “Signal tower reaches the coast, not Brackenmill. The courier might carry a written warning inland, though feet are slower than fear.”

**“Why should we trust you?”**

> “You shouldn't yet. Trust the dry shed, the lifting tackle, and the fact I came down where you could see me.”

### Reading Mara

**DC 10 Insight:** She is wary, not afraid, and sincerely offering limited help.  
**DC 14 Insight:** She is concealing a personal omen, not knowledge of Olive or the puppet master.  
**Failure:** The character still sees she has no weapon in hand and could have brought others but did not. Ask what would make them comfortable.

## Establishing Urgency Without Forcing Departure

Use two or three of these, not all at once:

- The Hearthstone's interval shortens once during the act.
- Pell's diagnostic lens shows the resonance is active and slowly intensifying.
- Speaking Olive's name produces a pained double pulse.
- Mara reports the dawn knot omen, suggesting the effect may be spreading symbolically.
- The coastal courier leaves Tidewick near sunset, creating a useful deadline rather than a mandatory one.
- The incoming tide will reach the machine in roughly ninety minutes, encouraging a repair decision without demanding instant travel.
- Pell states the tradeoff honestly: leaving now is emotionally immediate; preparing could make later travel materially faster.

Never say Olive will die at a precise hour unless later material establishes it. Her condition worsens, but the exact limit is unknown.

## Player Choices and Transitions

Present at least three options and accept combinations.

### 1. Go to Tidewick now

Mara leads them by the dune path. Pell either remains for ten minutes to finish stabilization or comes after securing the machine. Transition:

> Beyond the dune, slate roofs gather around a narrow inlet. A bell hangs above the harbor road, still and dark against the afternoon sky.

**Benefit:** Fast access to information, food, boatyard tools, shrine expertise, and courier.  
**Cost:** The machine remains beachbound until help returns.

### 2. Finish stabilizing Gannet Nine first

Resolve one repair check. Mara offers to fetch block-and-tackle or waits, mending her net. Transition:

> The machine's amber lens steadies. Across its cracked compass glass, the needle points past Tidewick and holds.

**Benefit:** Better bearing and preserved machine systems.  
**Cost:** The Olive clock advances one step only if the scene becomes prolonged beyond reasonable repairs; do not punish ordinary roleplay.

### 3. Rest briefly on the beach

A short rest is safe. Mara shares food and local facts; Pell works quietly. Transition:

> For one measured hour, no one asks you to bleed again. The sea breathes, tools click, and beneath it all the Hearthstone keeps count.

**Benefit:** Recovery and one extra candid conversation with Pell or Mara.  
**Cost:** Advance the background clock normally and describe one subtle worsening pulse.

### 4. Begin overland travel toward Brackenmill immediately

Pell secures the machine above the tide line as best he can. Mara provides directions to the inland road and urges them to at least take water. Transition:

> Tidewick remains a scatter of roofs to your right as you turn inland. The stone's pull strengthens with every step away from the sea.

**Benefit:** Earliest movement toward Olive.  
**Cost:** They forgo immediate local resources, repair acceleration, and courier intelligence. This is valid, not a “wrong” choice.

### 5. Split tasks

One group accompanies Mara while another helps Pell. Cross-cut once between each group, then reunite at the boatyard or beach. Do not punish the split with danger.

## Likely Player Questions

**Can the Hearthstone communicate directly with Olive?**  
Not reliably. Her name can provoke a response, and strong resonance can transmit condition or emotion. It cannot carry conversation in this act.

**Can healing magic help Olive at range?**  
Ordinary healing has no remote path. A spell that removes curses or contracts may provide diagnostic information through the stone, but cannot automatically reach her across the distance. Reward expenditure with a clearer clue.

**Can we teleport or magically message her?**  
Resolve according to available spells. A message may meet supernatural interference: the caster gets the impression of thread drawn across the channel. Do not fabricate Olive's words while her mouth is sewn shut; a mental-response spell may carry fear, pain, location impressions, or silence as its rules permit.

**Is the puppet master in Tidewick?**  
There is no evidence of that. The resonance passes beyond Tidewick toward Brackenmill. Do not decide or imply the entity's current location or motive.

**Is Mara involved?**  
No evidence supports it. Her secret is the knot omen. If scrutinized, graded Insight confirms this.

**Can we move the machine?**  
Yes, slowly with leverage now, or efficiently with Tidewick's tackle. It is not a Strength-check bottleneck.

**Will the tide destroy it?**  
Not immediately. There are roughly ninety minutes before water reaches vulnerable systems. Stabilization or moving it a few yards removes the threat.

**What does Pell choose?**  
He wants to stabilize Gannet and gather Tidewick's aid because that may enable faster travel, but he follows the party's decision. He never uses Olive's suffering to overrule them.

**Can we return to the cave?**  
Yes. It is quiet and safe from combat in this act. A brief search can recover mundane gear or confirm that the resonance grows weaker underground; it does not reveal the puppet master's motive.

**What is Tidewick like?**  
A small working fishing settlement around a narrow inlet: boatyard, shrine, inn, signal tower, courier stop, and wary but practical residents.

**How far is Brackenmill?**  
Far enough that ordinary travel takes days. Set the exact distance to match the wider campaign map. Once stabilized, Gannet can follow the Hearthstone's home-bearing in an uncanny high-speed Hearthline Return; Tidewick's ceramic collar makes it smooth, while Pell can improvise the same function over several extra hours if the town is skipped.

## End State Checklist

Before ending the act, verify:

- [ ] The party knows Olive is far away at Pell's hut in Brackenmill, not Tidewick.
- [ ] They have at least one clear resonance clue about her worsening restraint and silence.
- [ ] They understand the puppet master's motive is unknown.
- [ ] Gannet Nine's condition and repair paths are clear.
- [ ] Mara has introduced Tidewick and at least two useful resources.
- [ ] The party has chosen a next action without being forced into immediate departure.
- [ ] No combat or simulated combat beat has occurred.

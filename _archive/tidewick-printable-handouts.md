# HANDOUT 4 — COLD CASE CLUE CARDS
### *[Deal FRONT face-up for free. Reveal BACK only on a successful Medicine or Investigation check, DC 12]*

✂--------------------------------------------------------------✂

### CLUE CARD 1

**FRONT (what you see):**
The wound is at a joint — wrist, elbow, or knee — never the throat, never the heart. There is almost no blood. The body looks less *killed* than *stopped*.

**BACK (DC 12 Medicine/Investigation):**
This wasn't a blade's work. Something loosened the joint the way you'd loosen a puppet's peg. The victim didn't bleed out, because nothing inside them was cut — only *released*.

✂ · · · ✂

### CLUE CARD 2

**FRONT (what you see):**
A brass tag, no bigger than a thumbnail, sewn into the coat lining. Stamped in small, precise letters: **CONTRACT No. 4**.

**BACK (DC 12 Investigation):**
Whatever claimed this body filed it. The number implies others — at least three came before this one, and the sequence isn't finished. Someone is still uncounted.

✂ · · · ✂

### CLUE CARD 3

**FRONT (what you see):**
The unclaimed body's fingers are too long. Counting closely, there is one joint too many in each.

**BACK (DC 12 Medicine):**
This was never a townsperson. Whatever this is, it was built or grown to hold strings — and by the wear on the joints, it failed at something before it died.

✂ · · · ✂

### CLUE CARD 4

**FRONT (what you see):**
Fresh scratches on the underside of the crypt slab, deep enough to catch a fingernail. The slab was pried up **from inside**.

**BACK (DC 12 Investigation):**
Something got *out* from below, not in from above. Whatever left the crypt is smaller than a person, or folded itself smaller to fit through the gap.

✂ · · · ✂

### CLUE CARD 5

**FRONT (what you see):**
A single black thread, snagged on the corpse's collarbone. It's finer than any local weave, and faintly warm to the touch.

**BACK (DC 12 Investigation or Medicine):**
This thread doesn't come from a loom — it comes from something that was *worn*, not woven. Holding it too long leaves your fingers stiff for an hour, as if they've briefly forgotten how joints work.

# HANDOUT 6 — TEDDY'S SECRET NOTE
### *[SEALED HANDOUT — for the player playing Teddy ONLY. Fold and seal before the session. Do not read this at the table.]*

✂--------------------------------------------------------------✂

> *This note is for your eyes only. What you do with the information is entirely your choice — including whether you ever tell the other players. Nobody at this table, including the DM, will force a reveal. If at any point this stops being fun for you, tell the DM privately and we will adjust or drop this thread — no explanation required.*

---

### WHAT'S HAPPENING TO YOU (in-fiction)

Something in your wrist answers the resonance stone's pulse. A faint *click*, like a marionette joint settling into its socket. Nobody else has ever noticed it. You feel watched by the empty air above your own shoulders — not menaced, exactly. *Rehearsed.*

You don't know why. You don't know since when. It has simply always been a little bit true, the way a scar is always a little bit true even after it stops hurting.

### SYMPTOMS (narrate these to yourself; the DM will cue you privately)

- Your wrist clicks softly whenever the resonance stone activates nearby. Only you hear it.
- Whistlecraft's marionettes turn slightly to track you, even the ones on strings no one is pulling.
- Mirrors and still water lag about half a second behind your reflection — once per scene, at most.
- If you touch black thread or black iron directly, your hand cramps, fingers curling like something is remembering how to close them for you.
- Once, if it's dramatic, your own shadow shows one extra elbow joint. No one else sees it unless they're looking for a reason to.

### WHEN DISADVANTAGE TRIGGERS (mechanics)

Roll with **disadvantage** on your very next ability check or attack roll whenever:
- You hold black thread or black iron in a bare hand for more than a few seconds, **or**
- You are the one who cuts, burns, or otherwise destroys a Contract tag, **or**
- The Collector (Mr. Strings / Ashur Vail) speaks your name aloud.

This is never meant to punish you — it's a flavor cue. If it's ever inconvenient for the story rather than fun, tell the DM and we'll skip it that time.

### WHAT TO NARRATE PRIVATELY

When one of the above happens, quietly tell the DM (a note, a whisper, a private message) that you're doing the "wrist thing." The DM will describe the sensory detail to you alone — the click, the lag, the cramp — without announcing anything to the table. Other players may ask what happened; you decide what, if anything, your character shows on their face.

### THE ONE THING YOU DO KNOW

Deep down, some part of you recognizes the word **understudy** the way you'd recognize your own handwriting. You don't know what role you're understudying, or for whom. You suspect you'd rather not find out on someone else's schedule.

### CONSENT NOTE

Revealing this to the table — partially, fully, or never — is **entirely your call**, at any pace you like, across any number of sessions. There is no "correct" answer and no clock forcing your hand faster than you want to go. This is your character's secret to spend how you choose.

# HANDOUT 11 — SESSION OPENING: STRONG START MENU
### *[DM ONLY — pick ONE before the table sits down. Never open with a blank "what do you do?"]*

✂--------------------------------------------------------------✂

### OPTION A — "The Hand in the Foam" *(sensory, atmospheric open)*

**Read aloud:**
> Tidewick receives you badly. Each withdrawing wave takes a little sand from beneath your feet, as though the shore has decided your footprints are an argument. Twenty paces downshore, a brass-and-iron hand the size of a cartwheel protrudes from the foam. Its fingers open and close around nothing. The resonance stone gives one low note — not quite sound. It is the feeling of glass remembering it was once sand.
>
> Cobb wakes on one elbow, looks at the stone, and goes pale. **"Nobody move it,"** he says. Then, quieter: **"No — move it if it burns you. Put it down if it sings. Throw it in the sea only if it says a name you love."**

**Immediate choices (offer, don't demand):** stabilize Olive · check on Cobb · investigate the iron hand · look toward the smoke and bells inland.

---

### OPTION B — "Low Tide, Ticking Clock" *(urgent, mechanical open)*

**Read aloud:**
> You surface into the scene already mid-crisis: the tide is coming in fast, and Cobb is pinned to the knee under Longarm's fallen brass forearm, cursing with the calm of a man who has done this before. The resonance stone, half-buried nearby, hums louder with every wave that reaches it. Cobb doesn't ask for help. He simply says, **"Any time now would be excellent,"** through his teeth.

**Immediate mechanical stakes:** Athletics/Strength to shift the arm before the next wave (no real chance of failure-harm — this is a "yes, and" beat, not a trap); whoever touches the stone first hears the first note.

---

### OPTION C — "Someone Already Watching" *(hook-forward, ominous open)*

**Read aloud:**
> You arrive to find the beach already disturbed. Fresh boot prints — long, narrow, oddly spaced — lead up the cliff stair and simply stop, as if their owner had been lifted away mid-step. A single black thread lies coiled in the sand where the prints end, still faintly warm. Cobb is on his feet already, staring up the stair, breathing hard.
>
> **"Did you see him?"** he asks, not looking away from the empty path. **"Tall. Gold at the cuffs. Tell me you saw him."**

**Immediate choices:** follow the boot prints · examine the black thread · press Cobb for who "him" is · tend to Olive, who has gone rigid at the word "him."

# Next Session Master Walkthrough: Strings & Salt Water
### A complete, run-at-the-table guide for an exploratory, roleplay-heavy Tidewick session

> **DETAILED REFERENCE — DO NOT USE AS YOUR PRIMARY TABLE GUIDE**
>
> The streamlined version is now organized by act in:
>
> `tidewick-next-session/00-START-HERE.md`
>
> Open that file first. It links to short Act One, Act Two, Act Three, clocks, NPCs, contingencies, and minigames.

This document consolidates the session bible, research, handouts, dialogue pack, contingency work, dry runs, and the six-agent live playtest into one canonical walkthrough.

It is deliberately written as a **walkthrough, not a script**. The recommended route is clear enough to run confidently, but every scene includes exits into other parts of town. The players decide which material becomes tonight’s story.

# 1. Session at a Glance

## The premise

The party has survived the resonance cave, sacrificed memories, defeated the cave’s enormous guardian, and watched Olive’s uncle Cobb finish the battle in his improvised Longarm mech. The cave expelled them onto a cold beach outside Tidewick with the resonance stone.

Tidewick is still recovering from:

- the overnight murders;
- the puppet-string attack at St. Wren’s Church;
- the disappearance of Olive’s parents;
- Olive’s eyes and mouth being sealed by supernatural strings;
- rumors that the killer is not merely a murderer, but an infernal collector.

This session is the breath after several combat-heavy sessions. Its threats are:

- unanswered promises;
- frightened townspeople;
- compromised trust;
- infernal legal language;
- the fear that rescuing Olive incorrectly could harm her;
- the possibility that Teddy is already connected to the same curse.

No combat is required.

## Recommended duration

This structure assumes a session of roughly **3.5 to 4 hours**.

| Real time | Expected play |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Beach landing and approach |
| 0:20–0:45 | First chosen town node |
| 0:45–1:20 | Second node / first substantial activity |
| 1:20–1:45 | Third node or Breath scene |
| 1:45–2:30 | One major optional branch |
| 2:30–3:25 | Player-chosen climax |
| Final 20–30 min | Resolution and ending image |

Do not attempt to run every location. A successful session will probably contain:

- the beach;
- three or four town locations;
- one minigame;
- one substantial social or ritual scene;
- one powerful ending.

## Recommended heart-first spine

If the players immediately care about Olive, the most reliable route is:

**Beach → Olive → Cobb’s workshop → Bram’s forge → short tavern Breath → one detour (Watch OR Whistlecraft OR church) → unbinding attempt**

This is not the “correct” route. It is the route most likely to produce an emotionally complete single session.

## Other fully valid session shapes

### Investigation-first

**Beach → church → Watch → mortuary → Cobb → ledger revelation**

The session ends with the party understanding the numbered Contract system, even if Olive remains bound.

### Promise-first

**Beach → Bram → Cobb/Longarm → tavern → Olive → partial ritual**

The party honors its relationships before confronting the curse.

### Town-life-first

**Beach → market → tavern games → rumors → Watch or Whistlecraft → Olive crisis enters their current scene**

The session remains successful even if Olive is not their first destination.

### Coast-first

**Beach → docks → lighthouse → drowned chapel → Cobb**

The party explores the stone’s resonance and discovers language capable of disputing the Collector’s claim.

# 2. Canonical GM Truths

These are not opening exposition. They are your internal answers so that improvisation remains consistent.

## Ashur Vail

Ashur Vail, called **Mr. Strings**, is Hell’s highest-ranked bounty hunter. His formal title is **First of the Stringed Ledger**.

He is not ranked first because he kills the most people. He is ranked first because his collections are theatrical, technically legal under infernal law, and emotionally devastating.

His core drive:

> Collect outstanding performances with perfect style, and make the condemned applaud their own damnation.

He should not physically appear this session. His presence is expressed through:

- Contract tags;
- legal notices;
- puppets;
- applause from empty places;
- rearranged objects;
- voices from pipes, bells, or paper;
- the word **understudy**.

## Cobb’s old promise

Years ago, Cobb survived a workshop collapse through infernal assistance. In desperation he promised:

> “My hands, and all labor performed through them, until the debt is discharged.”

Cobb believed he had promised only his own labor.

Later, Cobb used Longarm—and his own hands—to save Olive during an accident. Ashur’s ledger now classifies Olive’s continued life as “labor performed through Cobb’s hands.” Her parents objected and were taken as witnesses or guarantors.

The contract is coercive and contestable. Its weakness is that:

- Olive never consented;
- Cobb did not understand the hidden interpretation;
- the “beneficiary” was a child;
- refusal was deliberately silenced;
- the contract confuses a person with Cobb’s work.

The party does not need to learn every detail tonight.

## Why Olive’s eyes and mouth are sealed

The eyes prevent her from acting as a witness.

The mouth prevents her from refusing the claim.

The strings are not merely restraints. They are teaching the surrounding world to treat her obedience as normal.

## What the resonance stone does

The stone does not answer direct questions.

It **harmonizes with what a person, object, or place is trying not to remember**.

It can:

- replay emotional fragments;
- recognize matching wounds or materials;
- reveal that two objects share a history;
- store and release a sound;
- quiet a room by absorbing its rhythm;
- help separate Olive’s real memory from the Collector’s imposed claim.

## Teddy’s secret

Teddy has been marked as an **understudy**.

Do not decide tonight exactly what role that means unless Teddy’s player actively pursues it. It may mean:

- a replacement collector;
- a replacement puppet;
- a backup target;
- someone capable of inheriting Ashur’s strings;
- a person unknowingly rehearsed to betray the party.

The ambiguity is useful.

Use a maximum of **two Teddy breadcrumbs** tonight.

# 3. Non-Negotiable Running Rules

Keep these beside you.

## Rule 1: Three or four active hooks only

The entire town is open, but not every hook should demand attention simultaneously.

Start with:

1. Help Olive.
2. Return to Bram.
3. Investigate the murders.
4. Understand the stone.

Neri, the drowned chapel, the missing boat, and the infernal poster begin dormant. Activate them only through player curiosity.

## Rule 2: Essential clues are free

If the party is in the correct place and asks a reasonable question, give the essential clue.

Rolls provide:

- additional interpretation;
- leverage;
- faster results;
- reduced cost;
- useful secondary details.

Do not hide these facts behind a failed check:

- the deaths occurred on a schedule;
- the tags are numbered contracts;
- Olive’s binding is a claim, not ordinary thread;
- consent matters to the unbinding;
- Ashur’s legal standing can be challenged.

## Rule 3: Teddy has a shared breadcrumb budget

Write:

**TEDDY: ☐ ☐**

Once two tells have occurred, suppress every other Teddy tell for the night.

Potential tells:

- wrist-click near the stone;
- black iron’s two-note resonance;
- marionettes tracking him;
- hand cramp from black thread;
- Olive looking at him after being freed;
- a Ledger tick producing a private sensation.

Do not use three of these in quick succession.

## Rule 4: Ask who carries the stone

The live playtest exposed an important issue: when the party splits, the cure tool can accidentally leave with the investigation group.

Whenever the party divides, explicitly ask:

> “Before we split the scene: who is carrying the resonance stone?”

Default it to the Olive/Cobb group only if nobody cares.

If another group carries it, that is valid. Olive can still communicate and Cobb can still prepare, but any ritual requires reunion.

## Rule 5: Olive is a person, not a puzzle

Olive is:

- clever;
- stubborn;
- observant through sound and vibration;
- capable of testing whether people respect her;
- frightened but not passive;
- allowed to reject help.

The strings are the problem. Olive is not “broken.”

## Rule 6: Clocks transform play; they do not punish curiosity

If the party ignores Olive because it is having fun elsewhere, do not punish the players for choosing the sandbox.

Bring the consequence into the activity they chose.

Examples:

- At the tavern, Olive appears at the doorway, drawn by the self-ringing bell.
- At Bram’s forge, the completed weapon rings and points toward St. Wren’s.
- At the docks, every line in the fishermen’s nets pulls toward Loom Street.

The consequence creates the next playable situation.

## Rule 7: One substantial Score at a time

A **Score** is a committed scene:

- completing the weapons;
- trading evidence with Halloway;
- clearing Neri;
- exploring the drowned chapel;
- performing the unbinding.

After a Score, run a short **Breath**:

- show one consequence;
- tick relevant clocks;
- let one NPC react;
- let the party talk;
- ask where they go next.

# 4. Your Trackers

## Olive’s Stillness: 6 segments

Tick after two full nodes in which the party knowingly avoids Olive or her condition.

Do not tick while they are:

- seeking ritual components;
- working with Cobb;
- recovering the crank;
- investigating a clue explicitly tied to her.

Suggested manifestations:

| Fill | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Purdy says Olive has stopped tapping responses |
| 2 | Her music box turns once without a crank |
| 3 | Olive begins hearing the church bell continuously |
| 4 | Black thread appears around one shoe |
| 5 | She leaves the cottage, moving toward St. Wren’s |
| 6 | Her crisis enters the party’s current scene |

At 6, do not injure her automatically. Transform the scene.

## Town Panic: 8 segments

Tick for:

- public magical scares;
- threats against civilians;
- openly defying the Watch;
- the stone shrieking in a crowd;
- announcing that Hell owns the town without offering hope;
- a public ritual that goes visibly wrong.

Do not tick for:

- private supernatural discoveries;
- honest grief;
- townsfolk learning useful information;
- quiet failures.

## Hell’s Ledger: 6 segments

Tick only when:

1. a physical Contract tag is found;
2. “Ashur Vail” or “Mr. Strings” is spoken aloud;
3. the party creates a public spectacle connected to the curse;
4. someone forms a binding agreement with a hell notice.

Do not tick for generic rumors about Hell.

Suggested effects:

| Fill | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | A receipt appears under an object |
| 2 | Applause sounds once from an empty alley |
| 3 | A notice rewrites one sentence |
| 4 | A puppet adopts a PC’s clothing |
| 5 | A Contract tag appears blank, waiting |
| 6 | Ashur’s calling card appears in a place the party just secured |

## Mystery countdown

Check this every 45 real minutes or after every Score.

| Step | If the party has not altered events |
|---|---|
| Day | Town functions nervously |
| Shadows | Puppet dressed as a known NPC appears |
| Sunset | Church bell rope moves publicly |
| Dusk | Watch pressure rises around Neri |
| Nightfall | Whistlecraft puppets all face outward |
| Midnight | Calling card, applause, or Olive’s transformed crisis |

The countdown is what would happen without intervention—not a required plot.

# 6. Act One: The Beach
### Target time: 15–20 minutes

## Scene purpose

The opening must accomplish four things:

1. Reorient the players after the cave.
2. Make the stone feel interesting rather than merely necessary.
3. give Teddy one private tell;
4. point toward town without ordering them there.

## Read aloud

> Tidewick receives you badly.
>
> Each withdrawing wave steals sand from beneath your feet, as though the shore has decided your footprints are an argument. Twenty paces downshore, a brass-and-iron hand the size of a cartwheel protrudes from the foam. Its fingers open and close around nothing.
>
> The air smells of salt, hot oil, and a struck match.
>
> Between you lies the resonance stone. It gives one low note—not quite sound. It is the feeling of glass remembering it was once sand.
>
> Cobb wakes on one elbow, sees the stone, and goes pale.
>
> “Nobody move it.”

Pause.

Then Cobb corrects himself:

> “No. Move it if it burns you. Put it down if it sings. Throw it into the sea only if it says a name you love.”

## Teddy note

Privately give Teddy:

> As Longarm’s brass fingers click in the retreating surf, your own wrist answers. One tiny mechanical clench. Nobody else appears to hear it. You feel watched from the empty air above your shoulder.

Mark Teddy breadcrumb 1.

## Let each player act

Common actions:

### They check Cobb

He has bruised or cracked ribs, but can walk with help.

> “Bruised ribs. Pride punctured through. Longarm took the cliff personally.”

### They salvage Longarm

Let them retrieve:

- one massive brass hand;
- the control yoke;
- a cracked resonance gauge;
- one funny, harmless mechanism that keeps trying to salute.

No check is required to save the hand. A check determines whether they also save a useful spare part.

### They inspect the stone

The stone:

- brightens under attention;
- changes pitch when multiple people focus on it;
- does not respond to direct questions;
- grows quieter if wrapped.

If someone attempts Arcana:

- DC 10: it is not generating sound; it is causing nearby materials to resonate.
- DC 14: the pitch changes according to emotional or historical similarity.
- DC 18: it is listening for matching wounds, memories, or materials.

Failure still reveals the first fact; it adds a headache or wrong emotional impression.

### They search the beach

Choose one discovery:

**Best general clue:**

> A wooden puppet finger washed smooth by the sea. Its underside carries a burned maker’s mark: A.W.

This points toward Ambrose Whistlecraft without forcing that route.

**Best Olive clue:**

> A water-damaged bottle: “…if the strings reach the sea, cut them before the tide turns…”

**Best Bram clue:**

> A black coin under wet sand. Dropped onto stone, it rings like a bell heard underwater.

Do not give all three.

## Cobb answers

If asked about Olive:

> “My niece. Clever enough to argue with a locked door until it apologizes. She has not spoken since the strings came.”

If asked about the stone:

> “It hears what places are trying not to remember. Don’t ask it questions. Give it something honest and see what answers.”

If asked what he promised:

> “I made a promise to a man who did not deserve one. That is mine to carry.”

Do not force the full confession here.

## Leaving the beach

The instant someone commits to town, end the beach.

Ask:

> “Who wraps and carries the stone?”

Record the answer.

Cobb’s exit line:

> “Town is that way: smoke, bells, forge. Tidewick doesn’t decide what strangers are until dark.”

# 7. The Approach: Lyra Quill
### Target time: 5–10 minutes

This is optional friction, not a gate.

## Read aloud

> The dune path climbs through salt grass and splintered fencing. A fisherwoman in oilskins hauls a wet net uphill by herself. She notices the wrapped stone’s muffled hum, stops, and touches thumb to tongue.
>
> “Things that hum out here, you throw back.”

## Lyra’s three useful lines

Use only what the party asks for.

1. **Lanterns:**
   > “Three lights on open water. No hull under them.”

2. **Long-fingered stranger:**
   > “Peddler came through asking about the girl with sewn eyes. Fingers too long for his gloves.”

3. **Missing boat:**
   > “Merry Widow went out three nights ago. Her bell came back. Boat didn’t.”

## If they dismiss her

She shrugs.

> “Belief’s free. Retrieval costs.”

Let them pass.

# 8. Opening Tidewick
### Target time: 3–5 minutes

## Read aloud

> You crest the rise.
>
> Tidewick descends toward a tidal estuary in crooked roofs and salt-grey lanes. Forge smoke rises from Tallow Row. St. Wren’s steeple leans over Low Mercy. Fishing masts crowd the quay. A tavern bell rocks beneath a sign painted with a drowned ship.
>
> On Loom Street, one cottage has every curtain closed.
>
> On Red-Skein Hill, dozens of little wooden faces fill a puppet shop window.

Hand over the map.

Give each player one rumor card if you have not already.

Then ask only:

> “Tidewick is open. Where first?”

Do not verbally list all twelve nodes.

## If they debate for longer than five minutes

Summarize three roads:

> “You have three immediate directions: Olive and Cobb, which may take time but addresses the curse; Bram and the tavern, which honors promises and gathers allies; or the church and Watch, which risks political attention but may explain the murders. Which road do you take first?”

# 9. First Major Node: Olive’s Cottage
### Target time: 15–20 minutes

Run this early if chosen. Keep it precise. Do not let the scene become 45 minutes of repeating yes/no questions.

## Scene purpose

- Establish Olive’s agency.
- Show that the binding is intentional.
- offer the music-box sidequest;
- provide a fallback ritual component through consent;
- point toward Cobb and St. Wren’s;
- create a role for every PC.

## Read aloud

> The cottage smells of wool, boiled tea, and rain that never quite reached the ground.
>
> Olive sits beneath the window with her shoes pointed exactly together. Black thread runs from her closed eyes into her hair and from the seam of her lips to the collar of her dress.
>
> The thread is not taut.
>
> That is worse. It rests there with the casual ownership of a hand on a shoulder.

Mrs. Purdy:

> “Quiet, please. She hears floors better than voices now.”

## Assign parallel jobs

If one or more PCs are not drawn to the emotional interaction, offer:

- inspect the door and window;
- watch the curtain across the lane;
- examine the music box;
- check for tracks;
- quietly ask Purdy questions in the kitchen.

These jobs uncover real information.

### Door/window examination

No forced entry.

The window latch has been opened repeatedly from inside, though Purdy says Olive never opens it.

Across the lane, a curtain drops when someone looks toward it.

### Music box examination

Its crank is missing.

The spindle contains:

- herring scales;
- blue ribbon fibers;
- one grain of black sand.

These point toward Tobin and the harbor.

### Street watch

A child asks:

> “Are you the ones who’re going to fix Olive?”

Then runs toward Gullbone Common.

## Olive’s essential beats

Use three to five, not all ten.

### Beat 1: Measure

Olive counts the approaching PC’s footsteps using three thumb taps.

### Beat 2: Permission

If someone asks before touching her, Olive offers a white ribbon.

She loops it once around their finger, then lets it fall.

Purdy says:

> “She likes being asked.”

This becomes the fallback **habit/consent strand** for the ritual.

### Beat 3: The wooden bird

Olive offers a small wooden bird with:

- one broken wing;
- six tally marks beneath it;
- paint matching Longarm.

The bird proves Olive helped with Cobb’s work and is not merely his helpless niece.

### Beat 4: The drawing

Olive draws:

- a tall figure;
- gold at the cuffs;
- strings spreading overhead;
- herself inside a circle;
- a second unfinished circle.

Do **not** automatically aim the second circle at Teddy if Teddy already received a strong breadcrumb.

### Beat 5: The bell

A distant church bell gives one flat note.

Olive freezes, then scratches:

**one long line, two short lines**

This is a rhythm the drowned chapel and tide bell can later repeat.

## The Quiet Game

Use only three questions.

Communication:

- one squeeze = yes;
- two squeezes = no;
- tap = wait / wrong question;
- open palm = choice cannot be answered yes/no.

Do not require checks for respectful, understandable questions.

Call for Insight or Persuasion only if:

- the question is emotionally difficult;
- the wording could frighten her;
- the players want to interpret an ambiguous response.

Useful answers:

| Question | Olive’s response |
|---|---|
| Did the church attacker bind you? | Yes, then “wait”—the attacker served someone else |
| Is your uncle responsible? | No, then tap: his promise matters |
| Are your parents alive? | Open palm; she does not know |
| Does the stone help? | Yes, but she points to her heart first |
| Is the stranger still in town? | No, then points upward—not physically present |
| Is the music important? | Yes |

At roughly 12 real minutes, Purdy says:

> “She is tiring.”

Finish one final response and move.

## End the scene with choice

Purdy gives three actionable leads:

1. Cobb has a cutting device at his workshop.
2. Tobin near the harbor may know about the crank.
3. Olive reacts badly to St. Wren’s bell.

Then ask:

> “Where do you go, and who carries the stone?”

# 10. Second Major Node: Cobb’s Workshop
### Target time: 20–30 minutes

Run this after Olive if the party wants to help her directly.

## Scene purpose

- Explain claim/memory/habit.
- reveal Cobb’s guilt without exhausting it;
- give a playful Longarm sequence;
- provide ritual fallbacks;
- point toward Bram.

## Read aloud

> Cobb’s workshop looks as though a siege engine lost an argument with a clockmaker.
>
> Longarm occupies half the room in pieces. One knee hangs from a ceiling chain. Copper tendons coil across the floor. Olive’s tiny painted handprints decorate the control yoke beneath layers of soot.
>
> Every remaining wall is covered in diagrams of thread tension, resonance, and human joints.

## First, give them joy

After the heavy Olive scene, offer Longarm’s test arm.

Cobb says:

> “It is safe if you follow my instructions, which historically has been Longarm’s least favorite condition.”

Let a PC:

- lift a rowboat;
- crush a rock;
- stack barrels;
- arm-wrestle the machine;
- use it to hang a child’s kite from a roof;
- accidentally make it salute.

No danger. This is a tonal release.

## The cutter

Cobb’s prototype is jammed.

Run **Marionette’s Heart**:

1. Misaligned gear—Sleight of Hand or tool use.
2. Tangled internal thread—Investigation or creative manipulation.
3. Overtight spring—Perception, Insight, or physical restraint.

DC 12.

Failure does not stop the device. It gives it a side effect:

- sparks;
- a scream-like resonance;
- a tendency to cut the second thing after the target;
- a requirement that someone continuously retune it.

## Cobb’s explanation

> “A binding like this is a braid. Claim. Memory. Habit.”

Then explain:

- **Claim:** Why the Collector thinks Olive can be taken.
- **Memory:** Who Olive was before the binding.
- **Habit:** A freely chosen action stronger than obedience.

> “Cut the thread without the rest and the claim simply finds somewhere deeper to live.”

## Cobb’s confession—first layer

If pressed:

> “I promised my hands and the labor performed through them. I thought that meant work. Machines. Repairs. I did not understand how broadly Hell defines labor.”

Do not yet reveal every detail unless the party creates a safe, accountable conversation.

## Fallback components

Cobb always has:

### Fallback claim

A torn scrap of brass tag found near Olive’s window.

Using only this caps the ritual at partial freedom.

### Fallback memory

Purdy remembers Olive laughing when Longarm’s first hand fell off during testing.

Using only secondhand memory caps the ritual at partial freedom.

### Fallback habit

Olive’s ribbon consent.

This is valid but weaker than restoring her music and letting her choose how to play it.

## Leads from Cobb

- Bram has the black iron necessary to stabilize the cutter.
- St. Wren’s contains the strongest claim residue.
- The stone may recover an unaltered memory near the tide bell.
- The old debt is written in a letter hidden in Cobb’s desk.

If they search the desk without asking, they can find the letter, but Cobb’s trust drops.

If they ask directly and show compassion, Cobb opens it himself.

## Exit

The most natural next destination is Bram.

Cobb:

> “He kept your commissions. That means more than you think. Take the cutter frame. Bring back a shaving of black iron. Do not bring the whole ingot into Olive’s house.”

# 11. Third Major Node: Bram Ferro’s Smithy
### Target time: 25–40 minutes

## Scene purpose

- Pay off the party’s promise.
- provide a tactile minigame;
- deliver the gold-cuffed buyer clue;
- secure black iron;
- deepen town trust;
- offer a Teddy tell only if the budget allows.

## Read aloud

> The forge is warm but not roaring. Its heat has been portioned like food in a famine.
>
> Along the rear wall, weapons wait beneath canvas. Each bears a name, measurements, and a charcoal sketch of the hand meant to carry it.
>
> Bram stands before them with a hammer across both palms. He looks at you, at the covered weapons, and away—as if checking whether hope has made a fool of him.

Bram:

> “Well.”

Pause.

> “You promised you’d come back. I kept the fire lit. Don’t make me melt these into nails.”

## Let the reunion breathe

Ask each relevant PC:

> “What did you promise Bram the weapon would be used for?”

Bram listens closely.

This answer influences the weapon’s personality:

- protection: rings near restrained or controlled allies;
- vengeance: warms near Ashur’s contracts;
- precision: can cut supernatural thread without cutting flesh once;
- mercy: grants advantage on one attempt to restrain rather than kill.

Keep any benefit small and story-focused.

## Temper & Strike

Three stages:

1. Heat.
2. Fold.
3. Quench.

At each stage, ask for:

- one described action;
- one true sentence spoken to the steel;
- one relevant check.

Possible checks:

- Athletics;
- smith’s tools;
- Arcana for heat control;
- Performance for rhythmic hammering;
- Survival for quench mixture;
- Religion if treating it as a vow.

Base DC 13.

Results:

- 3 successes: masterwork narrative property.
- 2: excellent ordinary weapon.
- 0–1: functional but visibly unusual; Bram includes a compensating favor.

Bram never humiliates them for failure.

> “Again. The iron is not insulted. Yet.”

## Black iron

Bram unwraps it without touching it.

> “That is a bad night pretending to be metal.”

He explains:

> “Tall customer. Gold thread at the cuffs. Bought child-sized carving knives. Paid with a coin that rang underwater.”

If asked why:

> “He said a puppet troupe needed them. I wanted the coin off my counter. That is not an excuse. It is the shape of my mistake.”

## Teddy breadcrumb decision

If Teddy has used only one breadcrumb and the player appears to enjoy the secret:

- the stone makes two notes near the iron;
- Teddy privately feels the second note in his wrist.

Mark Teddy 2/2 and suppress all later tells.

Do not then have Whistlecraft’s puppets visibly track Teddy tonight.

If Teddy’s budget is already full, the iron simply resonates with the stone.

## Reward

Bram provides:

- finished weapons;
- a shaving of black iron for Cobb;
- the strange black coin;
- his testimony for Halloway;
- a promise to attend Olive’s ritual if invited.

## Exit line

> “Take the iron to Cobb. Take the truth to Halloway. Take the weapons wherever you intended. Those are three different obligations.”

The party chooses.

# 12. Breath Scene: The Drowned Bell
### Target time: 10–20 minutes

Use after a substantial scene such as the forge.

## Read aloud

> The Drowned Bell is warmer than it has any right to be.
>
> A ship’s bell hangs over the hearth. A corner table faces the wall and remains conspicuously empty. Missing-person notices cover one corkboard, some fresh and some yellow with age.
>
> Sela Marrow sets down cups before anyone orders.
>
> “You look like the cave lost an argument.”

## Give them decompression

Let the party:

- eat;
- tell the cave story;
- compare clues;
- examine new weapons;
- ask about the town;
- play one short game.

## Recommended minigame: Ship, Captain, Crew

Each participating player rolls 5d6 up to three times.

They must set aside:

1. 6—Ship.
2. 5—Captain.
3. 4—Crew.

Remaining two dice are Cargo. Highest Cargo wins.

Reward:

- one true rumor;

# 19. If a Player Bargains With the Infernal Notice

Do not improvise a permanent soul loss from one impulsive sentence.

## Step 1: The notice answers

> “Inquire within the quiet. State your consideration precisely. Vague offers void the contract.”

## Step 2: Ask for clarity

Out of character:

> “Is that a genuine offer your character intends to make, or are they probing the notice?”

Probing is safe.

## Step 3: Weight the consideration

| Offer | Result |
|---|---|
| Joke, vague promise, obvious bluff | Void; paper cools |
| Coin, minor secret, small favor | Hair-thin mark; perhaps Ledger +1 |
| True name, years of life, another person, binding service | Visible black thread; Ledger +1; real future obligation |

## Step 4: Trusted interruption

Once per attempt, Halloway, Purdy, Roke, or Ambrose can seize the paper and break eye contact.

> “You do not get to take it back by being clever afterward. You only get to not do it next time.”

If the PC insists, honor the choice.

## Step 5: Bind

> The notice warms. A black thread stitches itself around your wrist. In the blank portrait space, an eye opens once, then closes.
>
> Nothing else happens.
>
> That is the horror. It has simply been filed.

Give the player the physical black thread.

## Step 6: Rescission

Before the next eleventh bell, Cobb or Ambrose can help annotate or void the filing.

This becomes a Score, not an unavoidable punishment.

# 21. The Unbinding Ritual
### Target time: 40–55 minutes

This is the recommended social/exploration climax.

## Location

Best default: Cobb’s workshop.

Advantages:

- cutter available;
- Longarm can stabilize the control;
- room contains Olive’s memories;
- Bram can strike a tuning note;
- privacy reduces Panic.

Alternative locations:

- lighthouse for stronger stone resonance;
- church for stronger claim confrontation but higher risk;
- square for public support and public panic;
- drowned chapel for strongest legal footing.

## Set the stage

> The resonance stone rests in a shallow basin of saltwater.
>
> Bram’s black-iron shaving lies beneath it.
>
> Cobb’s cutter hangs open above the table—not touching Olive, waiting.
>
> The black threads lift from her face as if the room itself has inhaled.

Cobb:

> “Olive. I am here.”

Olive gives a stop gesture.

Cobb immediately corrects:

> “Right. Sorry. I am here because you let me be.”

This is important. Cobb must demonstrate change.

## Assign five jobs

Give every PC something meaningful.

1. **Stone keeper:** maintains resonance.
2. **Claim reader:** states why the contract is invalid.
3. **Memory bearer:** presents Olive’s true memory.
4. **Control keeper:** manages cutter/Longarm.
5. **Witness:** asks Olive what she chooses and reports only her answer.

If fewer players, combine roles.

## Phase 1: Establish consent

Ask the party how it requests permission.

Olive:

- one tap = yes;
- two additional taps = slowly;
- open palm = choice needed;
- closed hand = stop.

If Olive says stop, stop. The stone goes silent but preserves all progress.

## Phase 2: State the claim

Ashur’s voice comes from a pipe, bell, or cutter:

> “Final notice. Olive is a listed asset under a valid promise.”

The party presents evidence.

Useful arguments:

- Olive never consented.
- Cobb’s language named his labor, not another person.
- hidden consideration invalidates informed agreement.
- refusal was deliberately prevented.
- a person cannot be classified as work.
- the harbor covenant binds only the promisor.

Do not reduce this to one Persuasion roll.

If the argument is coherent, it works.

A roll determines whether the claim:

- weakens quietly;
- lashes out;
- exposes a hidden clause;
- reveals Olive’s parents’ status.

## Phase 3: Return memory

The stone offers three fragments.

Use details established earlier:

1. Olive laughing when Longarm’s hand fell off.
2. Olive’s parent tying her shoes.
3. Olive turning the music-box crank and deliberately missing the third note.

Let the players choose which feels truly hers.

If they choose incorrectly, Olive rejects it. This is not failure; it proves she retains agency.

## Phase 4: Teach a free motion

The binding expects:

- obedience;
- smooth repetition;
- silence;
- externally controlled movement.

The counter-motion should be:

- a mistake;
- a refusal;
- a self-chosen rhythm;
- asking someone else to stop;
- beginning again differently.

Best default:

> Olive fits the crank into the box herself. The melody stutters on the third note.
>
> She turns it again without correcting the mistake.

If her mouth is still sealed, she taps:

**Again. My way.**

If her mouth has partially freed:

> “Again.”
>
> Then louder:
>
> “Again. My way.”

## Phase 5: The Collector pushes back

> “Consent noted. Consent is not cancellation.”

Cobb:

> “No. But coercion is not consent.”

Ashur:

> “Apology accepted as evidence of comprehension. Debt remains.”

Ambrose, if present:

> “A puppet has no debt. A girl does.”

This is the thematic thesis. A girl can make choices; a puppet cannot owe.

## Phase 6: Resolution

### Full freedom

> The six-fingered knot above Olive tries to close.
>
> Her chosen motion is not smooth. It is stubborn, uneven, and entirely hers.
>
> Every black thread tears with the soft sound of paper being unfolded.

Ashur:

> “You have not erased the ledger.”

Olive:

> “No. I wrote in it.”

She is fully free. Do not secretly leave her controlled.

### Partial freedom

Choose eyes or mouth based on the party’s method.

**Eyes:**

> The thread across her eyes becomes ash. Olive opens them, furious, looking above everyone’s heads.
>
> “Do not look up. He watches through the strings.”

**Mouth:**

> The thread across her mouth loosens and burns away.
>
> Her first breath is ragged and entirely her own.
>
> “Not his.”

### Stabilization

> Olive’s fingers curl. The threads loosen enough for her to breathe and rest.
>
> They do not break.

This is still a success. The party now knows the correct method.

## Teddy endpoint

Only if:

- Teddy’s player appears comfortable;
- fewer than two breadcrumbs have fired;
- Olive’s connection is the strongest ending;
- the scene has emotional space.

Olive looks at Teddy and asks:

> “Does it hurt when they pull?”

Then stop. Do not explain.

If Teddy’s budget is full, Olive looks instead toward the empty air.

# 27. Final GM Guidance

The session’s emotional question is not:

> “Can the party cut the strings?”

It is:

> “Can the party help Olive reclaim authorship over herself without turning her into another object they control?”

The town’s question is:

> “Will fear make Tidewick accept a convenient lie?”

Cobb’s question is:

> “Can guilt become accountability instead of secretive self-punishment?”

Teddy’s question is:

> “How long can a person hide a string before hiding becomes rehearsal?”

Ashur’s question is:

> “Can every relationship be rewritten as a debt?”

Let the party answer through play.

The most important practical rule:

> Follow what they care about, then make that choice matter.

# Tidewick Read-Aloud Expansion

This addendum supplies ready-to-run scenes and living-town support for **Strings & Salt Water**. None of the scenes are gates. Pause whenever players interrupt, accept their answer, and spend the next unspent line only if it remains useful.

## 1. Full Dialog Scripts

### Beach Landing with Uncle Cobb

**Props:** resonance stone, wet tool roll, Longarm’s half-buried metal forearm.  
**Read aloud:**

> Tidewick receives you badly. Each withdrawing wave takes a little sand from beneath your feet, as though the shore has decided your footprints are an argument. Twenty paces downshore, a brass-and-iron hand the size of a cartwheel protrudes from the foam. Its fingers open and close around nothing. The air smells of salt, hot oil, and a struck match.
>
> The resonance stone gives one low note. It is not quite sound. It is the feeling of a glass remembering that it was sand.

*Cobb wakes on one elbow, looks at the stone, and goes pale.*

**Cobb:** “Nobody move it.”

*A beat. He swallows.*

**Cobb:** “No. Move it if it burns you. Put it down if it sings. Throw it into the sea only if it says a name you love.”

**If asked whether he is injured:**

**Cobb:** “Bruised ribs. Pride punctured clean through. Longarm took the cliff personally.”  
*He points toward the metal hand.*  
“My fault. I told her to brace for a fall. She braced for an argument.”

**If asked what Longarm is:**

**Cobb:** “A loader, a lifter, a coastal-rescue frame, and my most expensive mistake. Her proper name is Cobb Renner’s Experimental Coastal-Rescue Articulated Frame, which is why she is called Longarm.”  
*He listens to the hand’s slow clench.*  
“Her left knee will be in a gorse bush. Her temper is in that forearm. Do not kick either.”

**If shown a puppet finger, black coin, or Contract tag:**

**Cobb:** “Don’t say ‘puppet’ like it is a ghost story. Ghost stories make people careless.”  
*His thumb rubs an old notch in his caliper.*  
“There is a collector. A clerk with a knife, if you prefer. He finds a debt, decides who owes it, and makes the world agree. Olive is caught in his paperwork.”

**If asked about Olive:**

**Cobb:** “My niece. Thirteen. Clever. Stubborn enough to argue with a locked door until it apologizes. She has not spoken since the strings came.”  
*He cannot meet anyone’s eyes for a moment.*  
“I came for a cutter: something that could cut a claim without cutting the person claimed. I found that stone instead.”

**If asked about the resonance stone:**

**Cobb:** “Not a jewel. Not a battery. It hears what places are trying not to remember. It was under the cave, yes? Then it heard something there. Now it has heard us.”  
*He looks toward the inland smoke.*  
“Do not ask it questions. Give it something honest and see what answers.”

**If pressed about his own debt:**

**Cobb:** “I made a promise to a man who did not deserve one. That is mine to carry.”  
*Then, quieter:*  
“Find a brass tag, bring it to me before you burn it. Please.”

**If the party wants to repair Longarm:**

**Cobb:** “Workshop has tools. Bram Ferro has iron. Tidewick has both, though it has lately become possessive of them. We can make Longarm walk again.”  
*A brief grin.*  
“Or leave her here. She will be furious, but she has been furious before.”

**Exit line when they turn inland:**

**Cobb:** “Town is that way: smoke, bells, forge. Tidewick does not decide what strangers are until dark.”  
*He rises with a wince.*  
“Come on. If the stone hums twice, tell me. Twice means it has found a matching wound.”

**DM note:** Near Teddy, narrate the second note slightly sharp and give Teddy the private wrist-click. Cobb knows no more than the party.

### First Meeting Olive: Ten Nonverbal Beats

**Read aloud:**

> The cottage smells of wool, boiled tea, and rain that never quite reached the ground. Olive sits beneath the window with her shoes pointed exactly together. Black thread runs from her closed eyes into her hair and from the seam of her lips to the collar of her dress. It is not taut. That is worse: it rests there with the casual ownership of a hand on a shoulder.

Run each beat slowly. A player can respond, investigate, comfort her, or leave after any beat.

1. **Measure.** Olive turns toward the first PC who enters and counts their footfalls with three taps of her thumb on her knee.
2. **Warning.** If anyone says “Olive,” she shakes her head once and points at the window. Across the lane, a curtain falls as though someone stepped away.
3. **Offering.** She holds out a tiny wooden bird with one broken wing and six tally marks scratched into its belly.
4. **Test.** Gentle acceptance loosens her shoulders. Rough handling, jokes, or demands make her fold the bird against her chest. Neither blocks the scene.
5. **Stone.** The resonance stone warms. Olive taps twice on the floor, puts one hand over her heart, and points toward the stone.
6. **Drawing.** She crawls to a table and draws a tall long-sleeved figure holding a web of lines. She circles a small child figure, then hesitantly adds a second circle. If Teddy is present, she angles the page toward Teddy.
7. **Music box.** She finds the blue box by touch, feels its empty crank spindle, and makes a turning gesture. She points harborward, then beneath the floorboards, then to herself.
8. **Permission.** If someone asks before coming closer, Olive puts their offered hand against a white ribbon on the chair. It loops once around a finger and falls loose. If no one asks, Purdy whispers, “She likes being asked.”
9. **Bell.** A distant church bell strikes one flat note. Olive locks rigid, scratches one long line and two short lines into the floor, and turns toward St. Wren’s.
10. **Choice.** She writes a clumsy capital **O** on the picture, closes the book to her chest, and waits. She does not ask to be rescued. She waits to see whether they decide she is a person before they decide she is a problem.

**Purdy, after a kind response:**

**Purdy:** “That is more than she has shown most folk in a month. Thank you for not making a spectacle of her.”

**If asked about the second circle:**

**Purdy:** “New is not always bad. But it is always worth noticing.”

### Bram Ferro Reunion: Promise Payoff

**Read aloud:**

> The forge is warm but not roaring. Its heat has been portioned out like food in a famine. Along the back wall, weapons and tools wait under canvas, each tagged with a name, a measurement, and a charcoal sketch of a hand. Bram Ferro stands before them with a hammer across both palms. He looks at you, at the tags, and away as if checking whether hope has made a fool of him.

**Bram:** “Well.”  
*He lets the word become nearly a laugh.*  
“You promised you’d come back. I kept the fire lit. Don’t make me melt these into nails.”

**If they apologize:**

**Bram:** “You arrived. In Tidewick, that qualifies as punctual.”  
*He taps the nearest tag.*  
“I finished what I could. Last temper needs the hand that will carry it. A blade ought to know whether it is for a guard, a butcher, or a fool with a heroic jaw.”

**If asked why the forge is low:**

**Bram:** “Watch bought half my coal. The other half went to shutters, locks, and men who think holding a spear backward improves courage.”  
*His voice hardens.*  
“I did not turn you away. I just did not have enough fire to lie about it.”

**Before Temper & Strike:**

**Bram:** “Gloves. Tongs. No speeches longer than a prayer. Tell the steel one true thing about why you carry it.”

**On a success:**

**Bram:** “Hear that ring? That is not strength. That is agreement.”

**On a failure:**

**Bram:** “Again. The iron is not insulted. Yet.”

**When the item is finished:**

**Bram:** “Take it.”  
*He gives it with both hands.*  
“It is yours because you came back. Not because you paid. Don’t confuse the two.”

**If asked about black iron:**

**Bram:** “That is a bad night pretending to be metal.”  
*He unwraps an oilcloth bundle without touching the ingot.*  
“A customer bought child-sized knives three weeks ago. Paid with a black coin that rang like a bell underwater. Tall fellow. Gold thread at the cuffs. No name.”

**If challenged about selling knives:**

**Bram:** “He said a puppet troupe needed carving blades. I wanted that coin off my counter.”  
*He points to the purchase slate.*  
“That is not an excuse. It is the shape of my mistake.”

**If the party wants the iron or a shaving:**

**Bram:** “Take a shaving, not the whole thing. Cobb has a locked box for it. Tell Halloway what I told you. I will tell her too, when I can say it without asking forgiveness before I earn it.”

### Ambrose Whistlecraft Explains the Collector

**Ambrose:** “Please do not touch the figures from above. They are balanced by temperament, and most people have none.”

*When shown a Contract tag, his hands stop moving. He turns it with a cloth.*

**Ambrose:** “Where did you find this?”  
*After their answer:*  
“Of course. The Collector has become untidy. Sit, if you can sit without demanding I make this simple.”

**If asked who the Collector is:**

**Ambrose:** “A collector is not a name. It is a job title. The creature wearing it now is Ashur Vail in ledgers that deserve to be drowned. On the street he is Mr. Strings. Children call him the Collector because children understand what adults pretend not to: some things take you politely.”

**If asked what he collects:**

**Ambrose:** “Debts. Promises. Unfinished goodbyes. A life promised away by someone who thought a promise could be private.”  
*He lifts a limp marionette by one string.*  
“He does not simply murder. Murder ends an account. Vail prefers an account that keeps paying interest.”

**If asked why Olive:**

**Ambrose:** “Someone wrote a claim around her. Her eyes are sewn shut so she cannot witness against it. Her mouth is sewn shut so she cannot refuse it. The strings are not controlling every movement. They are teaching the world to expect obedience.”

**If asked whether they can cut the strings:**

**Ambrose:** “You can cut thread. You cannot cut a claim by pretending it is thread. You need the thing that says the claim is valid, a memory of Olive before it, and a new habit stronger than the command.”  
*He looks directly at the party.*  
“A person must be allowed to choose. Hurried rescuers forget that.”

**If asked about the numbered tags:**

**Ambrose:** “Not a kill count. Sequence in a file: witness, correction, guarantor, perhaps. Do not assume a missing number means a dead person. The Collector likes a living signature.”

**If the marionettes track Teddy:**

**Ambrose:** “That five-fingered one is my self-portrait. I made it to remember a hand may be ordinary.”  
*He only looks at Teddy after permission.*  
“Patterns recognize patterns. Recognition is not guilt. Keep that distinction close.”

**Closing offer:**

**Ambrose:** “Bring a memory Olive loves, not merely proof of what hurt her. Bring Cobb only if Olive wants him there. If a red poster offers terms, read it twice. Then ask who benefits from every word you were not given.”

### Constable Halloway: Evidence Trade

**Halloway:** “Weapons peace-bound. Names, business, and one reason I should not log ‘vagrants with a glowing rock.’”

**If shown evidence:**

**Halloway:** “Useful is better than frightening, though I have had plenty of frightening.”  
*She writes as she listens.*  
“In exchange, you get one answer, one look at the map, or five minutes with the prisoner. Choose.”

**If they demand all access:**

**Halloway:** “No. You are not wrong to want more. I am not wrong to preserve a chain of custody while someone arranges bodies like evidence. Trade me something I can stand before a magistrate with.”

**If they choose the map:**

**Halloway:** “Five victims. Five sites. The thread runs from each to St. Wren’s.”  
*She traces it.*  
“It is a spiral, not a net. It turns inward. This empty center is Olive’s street. I have not told anyone.”

**If they choose an answer:**

**Halloway:** “The prisoner is Neri Vale. He says lanterns came over the water, a man stepped aboard without wetting the deck, and he ran. That makes him frightened, not guilty. I need proof before I can say it loudly.”

**If they meet Neri:**

**Neri:** “I did not touch Captain Sedge. I cut him loose. I cut the strings and he screamed because they were inside him.”  
*He grips his rope bracelet.*  
“There was a bell under the water. Tell Halloway I saw the bell.”

**If asked to release Neri:**

**Halloway:** “Bring a witness placing him elsewhere, the cutter he used, or a fact only the killer would know. Then I can release a man. Until then I can only refrain from ruining him faster.”

**If offered Bram’s description and Ambrose’s explanation:**

**Halloway:** “Gold cuffs. Child-sized blades. A physical source and a named pattern. That moves this from superstition to investigation.”  
*To Fenn:*  
“Write: ‘Neri Vale held pending corroboration, not charged.’ Big letters.”

**At trust two or more:**

**Halloway:** “I will be honest too. That poster was under my office door. It offers ‘courteous repatriation’ of an account. The portrait space is blank until you look in a mirror.”  
*She turns it face-up.*  
“If one of you is tempted to call that a deal, tell me before you sign.”

### Ritual Climax: Partial Freedom

**Use when:** They have one or two strands, or deliberately choose a cautious attempt.  
**Read aloud:**

> The stone rests in saltwater and sings too low to hear. Olive stands beside it, trembling without moving. Black threads lift from her face as if the night itself has inhaled.

**Cobb:** “Olive. I am here.”  
*Olive’s hand makes a stop sign. Cobb stops.*  
“Right. Sorry. I am here because you let me be.”

Ask who holds the stone, what memory is offered, and who asks Olive for consent.

*If asked, Olive taps once: yes; after a pause, twice more: slowly.*

**Collector’s voice, from bell or pipe:** “Consent noted. Consent is not cancellation.”

**If they reject the claim:**

**Collector:** “A charming objection. You contest the claim while retaining its benefit. Do you know what was purchased?”

**Cobb:** “No. But I know what it cost.”

Have Olive choose whether to pull a white ribbon. Then read:

> The invisible pull lifts Olive’s feet from the floor. She closes her hand around the ribbon and pulls down. One black thread snaps with the soft sound of old paper torn open.

Choose one result:

**Mouth freed:**  
> The thread over Olive’s mouth becomes ash. Her first breath is ragged and entirely her own.

**Olive:** “Not… his.”  
*She points at Teddy, if appropriate, otherwise at the space above the stone.*  
“Understudy.”

**Eyes freed:**  
> The thread over Olive’s eyes dissolves. Grey eyes open, furious and fixed above everyone’s heads.

**Olive:** “He is watching through the strings. Do not look up.”

**Collector:** “Intermission, then. Be proud. You have made the next act more expensive.”

### Ritual Climax: Full Freedom

**Use when:** claim, memory, and chosen new habit are present.  
**Read aloud:**

> The stone finds the tide bell’s note. Every loose thread rises toward one point above Olive, weaving a hand-shaped knot with six fingers.

**Collector:** “Final notice. Olive Renner is a listed asset under a valid promise.”

Ask players to state: the rejected claim, Olive’s meaningful memory, and the free act Olive chooses now.

**Cobb’s confession, if needed:**

**Cobb:** “I promised labor. Not her. I promised use of my hands after the cave collapse, and believed the words stopped at my skin. I was wrong. I hid the letter. I tried to fix it alone. I am sorry.”

**Collector:** “Apology accepted as evidence of comprehension. Debt remains.”

**A PC or Halloway:** “The claim names no consenting beneficiary. The witness was a child. The consideration was concealed. This is coercion.”

**Ambrose, if present:** “Then interpret this: a puppet has no debt. A girl does.”

Prompt Olive: “What do you do?” Let her response belong to the table. A reliable default is the music box:

> Olive reaches past the offered cutter. She takes the music-box crank and fits it into the blue box herself. The melody stutters on the third note. Olive lets it stutter, then turns again.

**Olive:** “Again.”  
*Louder:*  
“Again. My way.”

> The knot tries to close. Olive’s second turn is not smoother; it is stubborn. The room fills with the sound of a child insisting that a mistake belongs to her. Every black thread tears with the soft sound of paper being unfolded.

**Collector:** “You have not erased the ledger.”

**Olive:** “No.”  
*To the empty air:*  
“I wrote in it.”

> The stone falls silent. Tidewick’s bells strike midnight exactly once.

**Collector:** “Act Two begins when the understudy forgets his lines.”  
*To Teddy only, if welcome:*  
“Do try to remember yours.”

**Cobb:** “No questions. Not tonight. You can have tea. You can have silence. You can tell me to leave.”

**Olive:** “Stay. There.”  
*She points three feet away and starts the music box.*

This is a full victory. The Collector remains hostile because the party has challenged his system, not because Olive secretly remains bound.

## 5. Mini-Sidequests

### Find Olive’s Music-Box Crank (30–45 min)

**Hook:** Empty music box or Tobin asks whether adventurers return lost things “without shouting.”  
**Truth:** Tobin stole the crank because the box played his dead mother’s song.

At the seawall:

**Tobin:** “I didn’t steal it-steal it. It was lying there with no box on it. Then I put it in the box and it knew my mum’s song. Things aren’t supposed to know that.”

He traded it to Hob for a brass whistle. Hob put it in a herring barrel after it turned by itself.

**Hob:** “I thought it was cursed. Then I thought cursed things might pay rent. Then it sang about a girl I never met, so I put it in fish.”

Help identify one of six barrels now stored in the old net warehouse: inspect labels, question dockhands, use resonance, or help Hob unload. The correct loft contains child treasures and the crank wrapped in Tobin’s mother’s ribbon. The stone plays Olive’s melody plus a strange fifth note.

Nessa sees them leaving and suspects smuggling; calm explanation, Hob’s word, or returning a misplaced crate resolves it.

**Resolution:** Olive takes the crank and signs “later” by holding it to her chest. Reward: the box is a valid memory strand; Tobin’s bent compass points toward the drowned chapel.

### Clear the Innocent Dockhand Socially (30–45 min)

**Hook:** Neri in the cell; Tamsin brings dry clothes.  
**Goal:** Give Halloway three credible facts: Neri bought a candle at eleven, his cutter’s marks match rescue rather than murder, and a witness can vouch for him.

**Tamsin:** “My brother runs when he’s scared. That is his worst quality. It is not murder.”

Mara remembers Neri buying a blue memorial candle, but needs help deciphering her rain-smudged shell-symbol order slips.

**Mara:** “Blue wax for those who feared water. White for those who loved it. Red if someone was angry enough to keep living.”

At Eamon’s net pile, Neri’s cutter is caught in old rope. Eamon gives it up after help mending a section; its nicked edge holds thread, brine, and rescue blood.

Present Mara, Eamon, and cutter privately or publicly.

**Halloway:** “Neri Vale is released pending further inquiry. That means facts matter before fear.”

**Neri:** “I might run again. But not from this.”

Reward: two Watch evidence questions or night deputization; Neri grants boat passage and the drowned-bell detail. If Mara refuses, Tamsin gives Neri’s boot with thread residue, preserving a route.

### Low-Tide Drowned Chapel (30–45 min, no combat)

**Approach read aloud:**

> Low tide reveals three chimney-pots like broken teeth and a slick causeway to a chapel roof. Its bell tower is underwater. Every few breaths, a bell sounds beneath the surface, close enough to feel in your sternum.

Use a three-mark tide clock. At three, water covers the causeway; retreat by boat, rope, Longarm, or wet scramble—tense but not punitive.

**Room 1: Narthex of Names.** Inscription: **NO NAME IS PAID TWICE. NO NAME IS KEPT ALONE.** The door opens when a visitor speaks a name they genuinely choose to remember. If nobody does, the stone supplies one and the bell sounds accusingly.

**Room 2: Floating Pews.** Six symbols appear under drifting benches: shell, bell, hand, eye, mouth, thread. Three grooves wait at the far door. The solution is an agency order—hand, eye, mouth—explained in the players’ own words: ask, witness, speak. Choosing thread first raises tide one mark and whispers, “Command is not consent.”

**Room 3: Drowned Vestry.** A watertight cabinet holds a child’s recital pin, a harbor covenant, and a salt mirror. The covenant reads:

> A promise made without a heard refusal binds only the promisor.

The mirror asks each PC in a voice they miss: “What do you want returned?” Olive’s voice yields her laugh and a memory of painting Longarm’s knuckles. Truth yields Cobb hiding the original letter. The dead yields gold cuffs by water. Refusal safely shows the exit.

On departure, the bell plays Olive’s long-short-short pattern. A dry porcelain doll waits on the causeway: **CONTRACT No. 2: WITNESS RETAINED.**

Reward: covenant language for the ritual, one emotional memory, and proof that the Collector’s claims can be disputed.

## 6. Collector’s Bargain Micro-Script (POST-PLAYTEST PATCH T5)

**When:** A PC addresses a hell poster, courteous-repatriation notice, or blank portrait.

**Props:** poster handout; black thread/bracelet for heavy deals.

### Beat 1 — Boilerplate (always)

> A voice with no mouth: “Inquire within the quiet. State your consideration precisely. Vague offers void the contract.”

### Beat 2 — Weight the offer (DM-facing)

| Offer type | Example | Result |
|------------|---------|--------|
| Vague / bluff | “Whatever you want” / joke years | Void. No Ledger tick. Poster cools. |

### Beat 3 — Trusted NPC interrupt (once per attempt)

If Halloway, Roke, Purdy, or Ambrose is present, they may:

> *Grabs the poster / breaks eye contact / throws salt.*  
> “That’s what I meant. You don’t get to take it back by being clever afterward. You can only not do it next time.”

Converts impulse into a choice. If the PC insists after this, proceed to heavy binding.

### Beat 4 — Binding line (if they commit)

> The paper warms. A single black thread stitches itself around [PC]’s wrist, so fine it could be a hair. On the poster, where the portrait was blank, an eye blinks once and closes. Nothing else happens. That is the horror — it simply *files* it.

### Beat 5 — Rescission window

Before the next **eleventh bell**, they may void/annotate with Cobb or Ambrose help (Score). After that bell: deal stands until a later arc. Soft clock, not a gotcha.

# Full Walkthrough: The Note That Brings Her Home

**Length:** about 3 hours  
**Shape:** recovery and discovery → Tidewick sandbox → full-party rescue  
**Promise:** zero combat; every conflict is investigative, social, practical, or emotional  
**Primary files:** `act-1-the-beach.md`, `act-2-tidewick.md`, `act-3-olive.md`  
**At-table aids:** `minigame-rules.md`, `background-events.md`, `summary.md`

## Session truth

The party has recovered the Resonant Hearthstone after a brutal cave battle. Pell Arkwright's mecha-machine, Gannet Nine, lies on its back in the surf. The stone is resonating with Olive, who remains very far away at Pell's hut in Brackenmill. Tidewick is the nearest settlement and a place to breathe, make friends, repair Gannet, and understand resonance craft. Olive has never been in Tidewick.

The Bailiff of Strings—an infernal-contract bounty hunter and puppet master—took Olive's parents and afflicted Olive with supernatural twine. The session may establish his methods and authorship. It must not establish why the parents were taken, who hired him, or the ultimate contract terms.

## Before play: ten-minute setup

1. Put the six-segment clock from `background-events.md` behind the screen.
2. Put three physical tokens aside for Tidewick Favors.
3. Print or copy the trackers from `minigame-rules.md`.
4. Choose the party's Act 3 Standard, Hard, and Desperate DCs by average level.
5. Decide whether the campaign already has fast travel. If not, use Gannet's Hearthline Return.
6. Review the Act 3 safety statement. Decide privately what nonlethal substitute to use if the table does not want child-death stakes.
7. Select eight active Tidewick NPCs:
   - Mara Venn, practical guide;
   - Sela Rook, boatwright and Gannet collaborator;
   - Fen Coil, singer and frequency expert;
   - Brother Edrin Vale, careful contract interpreter;
   - Nims Calder, innkeeper and rumor verifier;
   - Della Rusk, auctioneer and game host;
   - Kett Vale, apprentice tied to the washed parcel;
   - Jory Fen, courier and departure clock.
8. Select one Tidewick social mystery. **The Customs Shed's Thin Note** is the best thematic default; **The Crooked Scale** is the warmest low-stakes alternative.

# Act 1 Walkthrough: The Beach

## 0:00 — Let the victory become real

Read the opening in `act-1-the-beach.md`. Then ask:

> “What is the first thing your character checks now that the danger is over?”

Do not insert a threat. Let them tend wounds, sit in the surf, retrieve gear, stare at the stone, or help Pell. The sea, broken machine, and visible fisherwoman provide motion without danger.

Advance the background clock to segment 1.

## 0:05 — The Hearthstone answers

Ask what each investigator actually does. Give one core clue for every distinct useful approach:

- **Direction:** inland, through and far beyond Tidewick.
- **Condition:** a living person experiences restraint, darkness, and forced silence.
- **Connection:** the signal catches on Pell and Gannet, pointing toward Pell's Brackenmill hut.

Checks improve precision; they do not gate clues. Use DC 10/13/16/19 as printed. On a low roll, give the clue and a harmless sympathetic echo.

When someone says Olive's name, pulse twice. Pell stops counting. Use:

> “Olive stayed at my hut in Brackenmill. If this is reaching from there…”  
> He looks at the machine, not the horizon. “We establish what we know. Then we move.”

If asked who did it, allow infernal-contract residue and the image of puppeteer twine. Do not provide motive.

## 0:15 — Put Gannet back in the story

Explain the practical choices before rolling:

- stabilize now in five to ten minutes;
- get Tidewick's lifting tackle and Sela's possible ceramic collar;
- improvise everything over several hours;
- travel by ordinary means over days.

Let one PC lead the stabilization and others help through distinct actions. Success restores a system quickly. Failure costs a panel or conductive wire but still stabilizes the machine. Ask the successful lead:

> “What does your character understand about Pell's machine that they did not before?”

Advance to segment 2 only after the repair beat or a meaningful delay.

## 0:25 — Mara comes down from the dune

Mara never sneaks up. She offers help before answers:

> “If the iron weather is yours, Tidewick has rope, a boatwright, and people who prefer explanations before smoke.”

Her conversation has four jobs:

1. establish Tidewick as twenty minutes away;
2. state plainly that Olive is not there;
3. offer Sela, Edrin, the inn, and the courier;
4. reveal the eye-shaped net omen after honesty or respectful curiosity.

Ask the party what matters most right now: speed, certainty, repair, recovery, or warning others. Whatever they answer, frame Tidewick as an optional way to improve that priority.

## 0:30 — Branch without punishing

- **Go to Tidewick:** use the default walkthrough.
- **Finish repairs first:** resolve one check, then Mara leads them in.
- **Rest:** grant the short rest; advance one clock segment and show a gentler worsening pulse.
- **Split:** cross-cut once between beach repair and town arrival.
- **Skip town:** Sela's collar is replaced by Pell's hours-long improvisation. Jump to Hearthline travel and give baseline Act 3 opportunities. Never make this the “wrong” route.

# Act 2 Walkthrough: Tidewick

## 0:32 — Arrive in a town that is already busy

Read the Tidewick arrival. Give the players the district map. Offer three simultaneous invitations:

- Della needs impartial help with two disagreeing fish scales.
- Odo's runner needs twelve hot buns carried past audacious gulls.
- Fen hears the Hearthstone disagree with a note in a net cord.

Do not list every shop yet. When players name a need, point to two likely places.

Use this recurring scene recipe everywhere:

1. a person is doing real work;
2. one overheard line gives texture;
3. tide, smoke, rain, bells, or light changes the senses;
4. an NPC offers an invitation, not an assignment.

## 0:42 — Run one welcoming minigame

Default to **Della's Honest Weight** for investigation and social fairness, or **Gulls and Tide-Knots** for comedy.

For Honest Weight, put three action tokens down. Ask for different approaches. Two successes prove damp wood, not cheating, caused the mismatch. Three also uncover Pip's unrelated tally change privately. Failure means Della pays the difference and the party can still help fix the counter.

For Gulls and Tide-Knots, place twelve tokens in a bowl and remove 1d3 after failed checks through three districts. Encourage ridiculous but harmless solutions. Odo feeds everyone regardless.

Award the first Favor visibly. Explain that a Favor can become a rescue supply, advantage, a truthful craft detail, or a mixed success later.

## 1:00 — Follow the party's appetite

Ask:

> “Do you want tools, food and rest, local magic, gossip, or to follow the singing-thread lead?”

### Tools: Sela and the shipyard

Sela treats Gannet as a craft problem, not a spectacle. Pell must collaborate rather than command. If respected, she reveals the salvaged ceramic collar. Let a PC notice Pell beginning to overexplain and choose whether to mediate.

Use Sela's line:

> “A borrowed solution still has an owner. Tell me what my collar will bear, and I will tell you what your giant is pretending not to weigh.”

### Food and rest: Nims at the Eelhook

Give each PC a menu choice. Nims repeats their exact unusual wording and keeps conflicting rumors on a slate. A candid account lets Nims correct the false claim that Olive is in Tidewick.

Ask one restful roleplay question:

> “What ordinary thing in this room tells your character the fighting is truly over?”

### Local magic: Edrin at Saint Orra's shrine

Edrin identifies coercive contract residue and warns that compulsion must not be mistaken for desire. He knows methods, not motive. A respectful comparison of shrine cords, witness-knots, and the Hearthstone can earn the True Interval boon.

### Shopping

Use the exact menus and prices in `act-2-tidewick.md`. Mention only items relevant to the stated need. Useful rescue choices include block and tackle, waxed canvas, listening cup, rescue line, rescuer's packet, witness-knot ribbon, and healer's kit.

## 1:20 — Run the thematic game

Default to **Knot-and-Note** with Fen. It foreshadows Act 3 without becoming a lecture.

Describe Fen singing a phrase and tying its rhythm into cord. Players may sing, tap, draw, sign, direct, or tie. Three rounds use DC 11, 12, and 13. At two Harmony, they learn tension makes the foreign twine vibrate. At three, they record a reproducible interval that can rescue a failed tuning check later.

If the party prefers physical craft, use **Ropewalk: Keep the Lay**. If they want civic roleplay, use **Hesta's Mussel Court**. If they want coordinated spectacle, launch the *Mercy Bell*.

## 1:40 — Give one rumor a human truth

Default mystery: **The Customs Shed's Thin Note**.

Lead it through people:

1. Della or Pip reports that only some people hear the whistle.
2. Kett admits opening a washed parcel, then properly surrendering it.
3. Anja shows the salvage record and requires careful handling.
4. Ysra identifies nonlocal fiber and metallic filament.
5. Fen reproduces the pitch; Lio compares short-short-long lamp flicker.

The resolution is documentation and safe storage, not defeating anything. The party learns:

- the writing is recent but uses archaic contract structure;
- knots may index terms or witnesses;
- the twine responds to tension and frequency;
- no evidence names its intended target, employer, destination, or motive.

If players pursue an ordinary mystery instead, let it be fully ordinary. Tidewick's debts, pride, hidden kindness, unsafe chimney, and apprentice anxiety should make it feel inhabited rather than constructed solely around the plot.

## 1:57 — Fifth Bell convergence

Ring the bell. Bring Anja, Fen, Edrin, Nym, Mara, Jory, and one befriended craftsperson together at the market steps or Eelhook.

Ask the party to state:

1. one fact they know;
2. one theory they do not know;
3. what warning Jory should carry;
4. which three Favors or material prizes they take.

Correct misinformation in character. Mara corrects Olive's location. Edrin distinguishes residue from intent. Nym distinguishes recent ink from old legal form. Jory repeats recipient and destination twice.

Read Tidewick's closing image. Ask each player which small object or memory they keep.

# Transition: The Hearthline Return

Describe the ceramic collar locking into Gannet, or Pell's improvised substitute if Tidewick was skipped. Use the travel read-aloud from `act-3-olive.md`.

During the montage ask:

- one player: “What did you carry from Tidewick?”
- another: “What promise did you rehearse for Olive?”
- Pell: “Which machine system are you willing to lose if the home-bearing slips?”

Advance the clock to segment 5. On arrival, stop using the background clock to determine Olive's fate. The visible Engine tracks now govern the rescue.

# Act 3 Walkthrough: Olive

## 2:12 — Calibrate the table and the machine

Read the safety statement before graphic details. Confirm Standard or Rescue stakes and any veil.

At the hut, Pell asks Olive before every touch. Establish:

- one tap: yes;
- two: no;
- three rapid: stop;
- held squeeze: trust/stay.

Ask Olive whether she wants to begin. She answers only after risks and the abort lever are explained. Persuasion can clarify; it cannot override her.

Put Resonance, Strain, Trust, Rounds, and Catastrophe tracks in full view. State:

> “Six Resonance can free her. Four Strain is a warning. Six begins an emergency, not death. You may pause or abort.”

## 2:16 — Give everyone a preparation contribution

Go around the table without initiative. Each PC can inspect controls, brace a station, read residue, establish Olive's limits, build a communication rhythm, or spend magic creatively. Use the preparation table in `act-3-olive.md`.

Assign or let players choose stations: Hearthkeeper, Flywheel Engineer, Thread Reader, Grounder, Anchor, and Conductor.

## 2:22 — Run the Engine

For every round:

1. narrate the new pulse;
2. let Olive communicate;
3. explain the hard choice and consequences;
4. hear every PC's distinct contribution;
5. resolve only the needed rolls;
6. mark tracks immediately;
7. describe the new state.

Use the round cards in `minigame-rules.md`.

### Round 1: Find Her Note

Offer gentle start versus fast lock. The important lesson is that the machine must follow Olive, not force her.

### Round 2: False Harmony

The twine mimics trusted warmth. Olive identifies it as false. Do not select a traumatic voice for her. The party chooses whether to spend progress to mute it.

### Round 3: Contract Hook

Show legal texture without motive. Let Olive state: *It pulls when you decide for me.* Present preservation, Hearthstone damage, or a consenting PC's temporary burden.

### Round 4: Unspooling

Olive chooses whether sight or voice is prioritized. Explain both mechanical consequences before she decides.

### Release

State the tracks and likely outcome before final choices. Let every PC describe a contribution but make at most three final rolls. If short by one Resonance, offer Trust, a cracked Hearthstone, or partial release.

## If Strain reaches 6

Say “Redline” aloud. Strain 6 is not death.

1. Offer abort, equipment sacrifice, or stabilization at Hard DC.
2. Failed stabilization causes Blackout.
3. At Blackout, again offer final abort, Pell's sacrifice, or Hearthstone sacrifice.
4. Restart is available only after explicit consent and restated lethal stakes.
5. The first Desperate failure gives another warning and another exit.
6. Only a second failure reaches Severance.
7. At Severance, one last willing sacrifice saves Olive.
8. Olive dies only if every exit and sacrifice is knowingly declined under Standard stakes.

Never surprise the table with this ladder and never improvise a faster lethal route.

## 2:48 — Let the outcome breathe

Use the outcome tier table. Pell's first question is:

> “Touch, water, quiet, or questions?”

Honor Olive's answer. She does not explain trauma on demand. If fully released, useful lines are:

- “I heard you every time you asked.”
- “He wanted me still. I wasn't.”
- “My parents were alive when he took them. I don't know why.”

Award only the number of clues the outcome allows. The best clues prove the Bailiff's authorship, route textures, or sealed contract structure without exposing motive.

Ask Olive where she wants to recover and who she wants nearby. Then ask every player:

> “What sound, gesture, or object helped Olive remain present?”

## Final image

For a full or partial rescue:

> Dawn has not come, but the workshop no longer feels like night. The Engine ticks as it cools. On the bench lie a fisher's knot, a shell chit, a scrap of copied music, and whatever the rescue cost. Olive chooses the first thing she sees—or the first thing she says—and nobody chooses it for her.

For protected failure, end on the Hearthstone carrying Olive's deliberate tap back to every waiting hand.

For death, veil the moment as written, then end with the party choosing what will be remembered about Olive beyond her victimhood.

Immediately fill `summary.md` while names, promises, costs, rumors, and Olive's exact choices are fresh.
